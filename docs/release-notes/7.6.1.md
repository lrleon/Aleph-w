# Aleph-w 7.6.1 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.6.1.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.6.1 is a patch release of the sorts and of the flows.
`introsort()` of an array sorts with pattern-defeating quicksort, in a
third of the time of `std::sort` on random numbers; the sorts of a
`DynArray` and `quicksort_op()` take the median of three items as their
pivot, where sorted or reversed input took them to a quadratic time or
to heapsort; ten sorts of the library that went through the standard
library go through its own; and Dinic, HLPP and the successive shortest
paths read with unchecked accessors where they keep their indices in
range, which with Clang takes more than a quarter of the instructions
off Dinic.

It is source compatible with 7.6.0, but for the changes listed in the
[Upgrade notes](#upgrade-notes). As with every release, recompile
everything against the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.6.1.
2. The sorts that are not stable leave the items that compare equal in
   another order than in 7.6.0: `introsort()`, `quicksort()`,
   `quicksort_rec()` and `quicksort_op()`. Neither order was specified.
   Code that needs one among equal items takes a stable sort, as
   `mergesort()` or `timsort()`, or a comparison that tells them apart.
3. `compute_stats()`, Mo's algorithm, Mo on trees and the splits of the
   R-tree sort with `introsort()`, where they sorted with `std::sort`,
   which is not stable either. Kruskal and the planarity test sort with
   `timsort()`, stable as `std::stable_sort` was: of two equal arcs,
   Kruskal still tries first the one that comes first in the graph.

## Fixes

- `quicksort()` and `quicksort_rec()` of a `DynArray` took the last item
  of each range as their pivot, so that sorted or reversed input took
  them to a quadratic time, and `introsort()` of a `DynArray` to its
  depth limit and to heapsort. `quicksort_op()`, of a `DynArray` or of
  an `Array`, took the median of the first, the middle and the last
  items, but moved it alone to the last place, which left the least of
  the three in the middle: reversed input took it to a quadratic time as
  well. In ranges of 7 items or more they put those three items in order
  and take their median.
- `introsort()` of an array could lose an item when the comparison
  threw, in its final insertion pass. The pivot stays in its place
  during a partition, and an insertion puts back the item that it holds
  out: the array keeps its items.
- `Examples/sort_benchmark.C` passed `timer.elapsed_ms()` and
  `is_sorted()` as arguments of the same call, whose order of evaluation
  is unspecified: with GCC 14 at `-O2`, the time of each sort included
  the check of its result. It reads the time right after the sort.

## Known limits

- **With GCC 13, a checked access may fall out of line in a unit that
  instantiates `quicksort_op()`.** The sorts of this release change what
  the compiler inlines in the whole unit. In a benchmark of a user of
  the library, built with GCC 13 at `-O3`, `most_negative_cycle_bounded()`,
  which does not sort, takes 10 % more time than with 7.6.0, as an
  `Array::operator[]` of its search is no longer inlined. With GCC 14
  and Clang 18 it takes the time of 7.6.0.

## Tests

- `Tests/sort_utils.cc`, for `introsort()` of an array: the order of
  `std::sort` on 8 patterns and 19 sizes around its thresholds,
  ascending and descending; pointers compared through their targets,
  strings, items that can only move, and a subrange; linear comparisons
  on sorted, reversed and equal input, and within 3 n log2 n against
  McIlroy's adversary; and a comparison that throws at each step of a
  sort, which leaves the items in the array.
- `Tests/sort_utils.cc`, for the sorts of a `DynArray` and
  `quicksort_op()`: the order of `std::sort` on five patterns and on
  sizes around their thresholds, and 20 000 sorted or reversed items
  within 2 n log2 n comparisons, where 7.6.0 took up to 200 million.
- `KruskalTest.TakesEqualArcsInTheOrderOfTheGraph`: with weights from 0
  to 2, the tree is that of a greedy pass over the arcs in the order of
  the graph.
- `ReturnDustTest.ReturnsTheDustOfAnEndFromItsSmallestFlowsFirst` and
  `TransportationTest.NotSquareKeepsRowsColumnsAndCost`, for the flows.
- `TimeoutQueueTest.Statistics` and `ResetStats` notify their condition
  variable with the mutex taken. The test could end, and destroy it,
  while the worker was still notifying: `ResetStats` ended so in a
  segmentation fault once, on macOS 15.

## Performance

`introsort()` of an array, in time against `std::sort` of libstdc++: the
least of 11 runs on one core, GCC 14 / Clang 18 at `-O2 -DNDEBUG`. In
brackets, `introsort()` of 7.6.0:

| Input | 10^6 doubles | 2·10^5 pointers |
|---|---|---|
| Random | 0.32 / 0.32 (1.05 / 1.04) | 0.39 / 0.40 (0.99 / 0.95) |
| 16 values | 0.23 / 0.22 (1.09 / 1.14) | 0.29 / 0.31 (0.95 / 0.99) |
| Sorted | 0.11 / 0.11 (0.76 / 0.82) | 0.16 / 0.17 (0.71 / 0.69) |
| Reversed | 0.27 / 0.26 (1.06 / 0.93) | 0.35 / 0.34 (0.96 / 0.90) |
| Sorted, 1 % swapped | 0.67 / 0.74 (0.72 / 0.71) | 0.69 / 0.69 (0.76 / 0.73) |
| Sawtooth | 0.51 / 0.49 (1.02 / 1.04) | 0.59 / 0.58 (1.00 / 1.04) |
| Organ pipe | 0.40 / 0.40 (1.10 / 0.99) | 0.38 / 0.36 (1.00 / 0.92) |

| Input | 10^6 records of 16 bytes | 2·10^5 strings |
|---|---|---|
| Random | 0.40 / 0.34 (1.04 / 1.05) | 0.84 / 0.61 (1.15 / 1.07) |
| 16 values | 0.32 / 0.27 (0.97 / 1.14) | 0.31 / 0.23 (0.95 / 0.92) |
| Sorted | 0.19 / 0.15 (0.77 / 0.75) | 0.05 / 0.06 (0.67 / 0.62) |
| Reversed | 0.37 / 0.26 (1.03 / 0.85) | 0.14 / 0.14 (0.83 / 0.80) |
| Sorted, 1 % swapped | 0.70 / 0.67 (0.74 / 0.76) | 0.49 / 0.49 (0.72 / 0.68) |
| Sawtooth | 0.60 / 0.51 (1.00 / 1.10) | 0.62 / 0.56 (1.15 / 1.13) |
| Organ pipe | 0.43 / 0.41 (1.10 / 1.14) | 0.67 / 0.61 (1.21 / 1.23) |

The pointers are compared through what they point to.

It compares 2n times on sorted input, 3n on reversed input and 2n on
equal items, where 7.6.0 took 13n on 10^5 items; and 2.7 n log2 n
against McIlroy's adversary on 20 000 items, against 3.75 n log2 n.

The sorts of a `DynArray` and `quicksort_op()`, in time on 200 000 ints
against 7.6.0 (GCC 14 at `-O2`, one core, the least of 5 runs):

- `introsort()` of a `DynArray`: sorted, 20.7 → 2.5 ms, and reversed,
  20.6 → 2.5 ms; random +1 %, and organ pipe −1 %.
- `quicksort()` of a `DynArray`: sorted and reversed, quadratic (146 and
  150 ms on 20 000 items) → 3.8 ms; random +3.8 %, and organ pipe +7.3 %.
- `quicksort_op()` of a `DynArray`: reversed, quadratic (49 ms on 20 000
  items) → 3.2 ms; random +1.5 %, and organ pipe +2.3 %.
- `quicksort_op()` of an `Array`: reversed, quadratic (9.8 ms on 20 000
  items) → 0.6 ms; sorted −16 %, random −7.5 %, and organ pipe −6.5 %.

On random and organ-pipe input the median of three costs a few accesses
to the `DynArray` per partition. Organ-pipe input still sends
`introsort()` of a `DynArray` to heapsort.

The sorts of the library, in cycles per call against 7.6.0 (perf, one
core, GCC 14 / Clang 18 at `-O2 -DNDEBUG`):

- Kruskal on 200 000 arcs: −3.5 / −37 % with random weights, and −6.1 /
  −46 % with weights from 0 to 9.
- `compute_stats()` on 10^6 doubles: 61 % fewer, with both compilers.
- The planarity test with its embedding, Mo's algorithm, Mo on trees and
  the R-trees: within 3 %, the noise of the machine.

The flows, in instructions against 7.6.0 (cachegrind, each algorithm
built alone at `-O2 -DNDEBUG`, on random networks with capacities in
quarters, tenths and integers):

|---|---|---|
| Dinic, floating flow | −0.14 % | −26.9 % |
| Dinic, integer flow | −0.14 % | −28.3 % |
| Successive shortest paths, floating flow | −2.8 % | −12.4 % |
| Successive shortest paths, integer flow | −0.19 % | −11.6 % |
| HLPP, floating flow | +0.71 % | +0.24 % |
| HLPP, integer flow | 0 | 0 |
| Cycle canceling | +0.10 % | +0.01 % |
| Ford-Fulkerson | within 0.01 % | within 0.03 % |
| Edmonds-Karp | within 0.01 % | within 0.03 % |
| Capacity scaling | within 0.01 % | within 0.03 % |
