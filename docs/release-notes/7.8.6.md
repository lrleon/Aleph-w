# Aleph-w 7.8.6 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.8.6.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.8.6 is a patch release. It fixes two flow networks that keep
pointers to their own nodes and arcs, which a change of the network
left pointing to nodes and arcs that were freed or that another network
had: `Net_Cap_Graph::update()` wrote the flows through them, and
`Net_Sup_Dem_Graph` kept its super source and its super sink after the
changes that took them away, so that `free_aux_net()` and
`is_feasible()` used freed nodes. And it fixes `non_feasible_nodes()`,
which gave two empty lists for a flow that is not feasible, in a
network with one super node alone.

It is source compatible with 7.8.5, but for a class derived from
`Net_Graph` that declares `disconnect_arc()` with another return type
(upgrade note 3). As with every release, recompile everything against
the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.8.6.
2. `Net_Cap_Graph::update()` throws `std::domain_error` when the network
   changed after `compute_aux_net()`: a node or an arc removed, an arc
   disconnected, also if it was connected again, a node or an arc
   inserted, a `copy_graph()` over the network, or its nodes and arcs
   exchanged through a reference to `Net_Graph`, by its `swap()`, its
   move or its assignments. It writes nothing then, and the auxiliary
   network stays, for `free_aux_net()`; another `compute_aux_net()`
   gives one that `update()` takes. In 7.8.5 it wrote through nodes and
   arcs that were freed, or that another network had, and took a
   network with nodes or arcs inserted. Another order of the nodes or
   of the arcs, as after `sort_nodes()`, is the same network, and
   `update()` takes it, as before.
3. For a class derived from `Net_Graph`: `disconnect_arc()` is virtual
   now, as `remove_arc()` and `remove_node()`, so that one that declares
   it with another return type no longer compiles, and one that
   declares it with the same signature is called through a reference to
   `Net_Graph` too. And `Net_Graph` has two new protected functions,
   which a member of the same name of the derived class overrides or
   hides: the virtual `swapped()`, which its `swap()`, its move
   constructor and its assignments call on each network whose nodes and
   arcs they exchange, and `swap_contents()`, which exchanges them
   without calling it. `swapped()` is final in `Net_Cap_Graph` and in
   `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph`.
4. `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph` forgets its super source and its super sink when
   they leave the network. After an assignment, a `swap()` or a move
   through a reference to `Net_Graph`, which take all its nodes away,
   after `clear_graph()` and after a `copy_graph()` into the network, it
   has no auxiliary network: `exist_aux_net()` is false,
   `free_aux_net()` and `is_feasible()` throw `std::domain_error`, and
   `compute_aux_net()` makes another. After a `remove_node()` of one of
   the two, through the network or through the base, it keeps the
   other. In 7.8.5 it kept their pointers: `free_aux_net()` and
   `is_feasible()` then used freed nodes, or `free_aux_net()` removed
   from the network two nodes that another network had. Its own
   `swap()`, moves and copies take the super nodes along, as before.
5. `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph::non_feasible_nodes()`, without the super source
   or without the super sink, lists every node that supplies and every
   node that demands, whatever the flow, as `is_feasible()` says that
   nothing is shipped then. In 7.8.5 it looked only at the arcs of the
   super node that there was, and gave two empty lists for a flow that
   filled them, towards a sink of the network or from a source of it.

## Fixes

- **flow** (#238): `Net_Cap_Graph::compute_aux_net()` builds an
  auxiliary network whose arcs keep, in their cookies, pointers to the
  nodes and to the arcs of the network, and `update()` follows them to
  write the flows back. After a change of the network those pointers
  were to nodes and arcs that were freed, or that another network had,
  and `update()` wrote through them (upgrade note 2). `Net_Cap_Graph`
  now marks its auxiliary network when a node or an arc leaves the
  network: it overrides `remove_arc()` and `remove_node()`, which are
  virtual, and `disconnect_arc()`, which is now virtual in `Net_Graph`,
  so that a removal or a disconnection marks it also through a reference
  to a base, and through `clear_graph()`, as in a `copy_graph()`; and
  `compute_aux_net()` keeps the numbers of nodes and of arcs, so that an
  insertion shows too. `Net_Graph::swap()`, which its assignments call,
  now calls the virtual `swapped()` on its two networks, and its move
  constructor on the network that it empties: `Net_Cap_Graph` marks its
  auxiliary network there. Its own `swap()` and moves, which take the
  auxiliary network along, use the new `swap_contents()` of `Net_Graph`,
  which does not call it. Counted by cachegrind on a chain of 1000 nodes
  with GCC 14 and Clang 18, from 7.8.5: in a `Net_Cap_Graph`, a removal
  of an arc takes 16 instructions more with GCC, which no longer inlines
  a helper of the removal there, and 1 more with Clang, +0.8 % and
  +0.04 % on removing and inserting again every arc; a removal of a
  node, +0.2 % and +0.04 %; a disconnection, 1 instruction more with
  each, +0.05 %; `compute_aux_net()`, `update()` and `free_aux_net()`,
  +0.02 % or less; a `swap()`, 8 and 12 more, +8 % and +11 %; and a move
  construction with the move assignment back, 6 fewer with GCC and 6
  more with Clang, −2 % and +2 %. In a `Net_Graph`, a disconnection
  through a reference takes 6 and 9 instructions more, +0.3 % and
  +0.4 %, and nothing more in a local network.
- **flow** (#240): `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph::compute_aux_net()` inserts a
  super source and a super sink in the network itself, and the network
  keeps their pointers; the changes that took those two nodes away left
  the pointers as they were (upgrade note 4). After a copy assignment
  through a reference to `Net_Graph`, a `remove_node()` of one of the
  two by the caller, `clear_graph()` or a `copy_graph()` into the
  network, `free_aux_net()` and `is_feasible()` used a freed node; after
  a move assignment or a `swap()` through the base, `free_aux_net()`
  removed from the network two nodes that the other network had; and
  after a move construction of a `Net_Graph` from it, `free_aux_net()`
  left the emptied network with 2^64 − 2 nodes. It now overrides
  `remove_node()`, to forget the super node that is removed once it is,
  so that a removal that fails leaves it in the network and remembered,
  and `swapped()`, to forget the two. Its own `swap()` and move
  constructor, which take them along, use `swap_contents()`. From 7.8.5,
  a removal of a node of a `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph` takes 15 instructions
  more with GCC and 6 with Clang, +0.3 % and +0.1 % on removing and
  inserting again every node, and 31 and 20 through a reference to
  `Net_Graph`, +0.7 % and +0.4 %; `compute_aux_net()` with
  `free_aux_net()`, 20 and 43 more, +0.1 % and +0.2 %. Its own `swap()`
  takes what it did, and its own move construction with the move
  assignment back 3 and 2 instructions fewer, −1 % and −0.7 %; through
  references to `Net_Graph`, a `swap()` takes 18.5 and 15 more, +17 %
  and +11 %, and the two moves 13 and 16 more, +5 % and +4 %.
- **flow** (#240): `non_feasible_nodes()` promises two lists that are
  empty if and only if `is_feasible()`, and without one of the super
  nodes it gave two empty lists, while `is_feasible()` said false, for a
  flow that filled the arcs of the super node that there was (upgrade
  note 5). In a network that only supplies or only demands, after a
  maximum flow, that happened in 572 of the 3080 runs of the exhaustive
  test of the supplies.
- **flow** (#240): `Net_Graph::swap()` leaves as it is a network that is
  swapped with itself, and tells it nothing. With the notice of #238, a
  swap of a network with itself through a reference to `Net_Graph` made
  `update()` of a `Net_Cap_Graph` refuse an auxiliary network that still
  mapped to it, and would have made a `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph` forget super
  nodes that it still has. The notice of #238 and this test cost, from
  7.8.5, 13.5 instructions with GCC and 11 with Clang in a `swap()` of
  two networks through references to `Net_Graph`, +12 % and +8 %, and 8
  with Clang in one of local networks, +8 %, where GCC takes what it
  took; and in a move construction with the move assignment back, 10 and
  12 through a reference, +4 % and +3 %, and 12 with Clang in a local
  network, +4 %, where GCC takes what it took.
- **flow** (#240): the overrides of `swapped()` of `Net_Cap_Graph` and
  of `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph` are final: an override in a network derived
  from one of them that did not call it left the pointers stale again.

## Documentation

- **flow** (#240): the documentation of `Net_Graph` warns that a
  network must not be exchanged or assigned, nor have an arc
  disconnected, through a reference to `Array_Graph`, whose functions
  are not virtual and know nothing of what a network keeps for its
  nodes and arcs (see the known limits). That of `Net_Graph::swapped()`
  says what a derived network is told, and those of `Net_Cap_Graph` and
  of `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph`, what a change of the network leaves.

## Tests

- `Tests/netcapgraph_test.cc`: 9 new tests. `update()` takes an
  unchanged network, the network in another order after `sort_nodes()`
  and `sort_arcs()`, and the network swapped with itself through the
  base. It refuses the auxiliary network, and writes nothing, after a
  `copy_graph()` over the network; after a removal of a node or of an
  arc, also through the base and followed by an insertion that leaves
  the numbers as they were; after a disconnection, also through the base
  and with the arc connected again; after a copy assignment, a move
  assignment and a `swap()` through a reference to `Net_Graph`, between
  networks of the same numbers; and after an insertion. And the mark
  goes with the auxiliary network in the moves and the `swap()` of
  `Net_Cap_Graph`. On 7.8.5, 6 of them fail, 5 with a use after free
  under AddressSanitizer.
- `Tests/tpl_net_test.cc`: `Net_Graph` tells once each network derived
  from it whose nodes and arcs a `swap()`, a move or an assignment
  exchanges through a reference to the base, and nothing to a network
  swapped with itself, and a `disconnect_arc()` through the base is that
  of the derived network. And a typed test of the pattern takes the four
  networks derived from `Net_Graph` in the library, `Net_Graph` itself,
  `Net_Cap_Graph`, `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph` and `Net_Cost_Graph`, through
  five ways of taking their nodes away through the base, with the other
  network alive and gone, and through nine that take some or none: what
  each network keeps is used or refused, its numbers of nodes and of
  arcs are those that its iterators see, and the sanitizers see nothing.
  The file does not compile on 7.8.5, whose `Net_Graph` has no
  `swapped()` and no virtual `disconnect_arc()`; without its first three
  tests, 4 of the 8 cases of the pattern fail there, those of
  `Net_Cap_Graph` and of `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph`, with a use after free.
- `Tests/net_sup_dem_test.cc`: 7 new tests. They take every way of
  changing the network, by what it does to the super nodes: it takes all
  the nodes away through the base, removes nodes, takes the super nodes
  along or leaves them; with each auxiliary network that a network may
  have, of the two super nodes, of the super source alone and of the
  super sink alone; through the network and through the base; and with
  each other network of an exchange. And `is_feasible()` and
  `non_feasible_nodes()` say the same with each auxiliary network,
  without a flow and after one, and with each super node removed. On
  7.8.5, 4 of them fail, 1 with a use after free.
- `Tests/graph_exhaustive_flow_test.cc`: a failure of each allocation of
  a `remove_node()` of a super node, of `free_aux_net()`, of a copy
  assignment through the base and of a `copy_graph()` into the network
  leaves the network remembering the super nodes that it has, also when
  the removal fails with an arc of the super node gone. It passes on
  7.8.5, which forgets nothing; a fix that forgot a super node before it
  removed it fails it. And the exhaustive test of the supplies checks
  the lists of `non_feasible_nodes()` in each of its 3080 runs, against
  `is_feasible()` and, with the two super nodes, against the balance of
  the flow of each node over the arcs of the network: on 7.8.5, 572 of
  them fail.
- `Tests/tpl_netcost_test.cc`: `TextbookNetwork` and `LargerNetwork` of
  the cycle canceling ask now for the maximum flow at the minimum cost,
  5 at 18 and 8 at 36, where they took any flow above 0. They pass on
  7.8.5, whose results were those already, and fail with a unit less of
  capacity into the sink.
- Of the 31 mutations of the lines that the fixes add, run on the tree
  of 7.8.6, 29 fail a test. The other two take away the `final` of
  `swapped()`, which no test can see: a probe with a network derived
  from `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph` that overrides it no longer builds.
- The 24 test programs and the 9 examples that include `tpl_net.H` pass
  in Release and under ASan and UBSan with GCC 14, and the five test
  programs that change, under the UBSan of Clang 18.
- The platform workflow passes: on Windows with MSVC, which runs 13 611

## Known limits

- Through a reference to `Array_Graph`, the base of `Net_Graph`, a
  `swap()`, a move, an assignment or a `disconnect_arc()` changes a
  network and tells nobody: `Net_Graph` keeps as its sources and its
  sinks nodes of the other graph, `Net_Cap_Graph::update()` takes the
  network and writes through freed nodes, and
  `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph::free_aux_net()` removes a freed node. It is
  documented, and left for 8.0.0, where the bases of the networks are
  looked at again.
- A network derived from `Net_Cap_Graph` or from `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph`,
  which the library does not have, is told of no exchange of its nodes
  and arcs, neither through a reference to `Net_Graph` nor through one
  to its base, and cannot override `swapped()`, which is final there: it
  must keep nothing for its nodes and arcs that such an exchange would
  leave behind.
- `non_feasible_nodes()` gives two empty lists, while `is_feasible()`
  says false, when no node supplies nor demands any more, which only
  the caller can bring about after `compute_aux_net()`.
- On `Treap`, `Treap_Rk` and `Rand_Tree`, the tree that the move of a
  `DynSetTree` builds for the set that it moves from makes its generator
  of random numbers, a block of `malloc()` of 2528 bytes, with any key.
  The `noexcept` of the move does not count it: without memory for it,
  the program ends. And the move of a `DynSetLinHash` or a
  `DynMapLinHash` builds a linear table, whose `DynArray` makes its
  directory with `malloc()`, and a default value that holds a key built
  by default. Both are left for 7.9.0, with a move of a tree that builds
  nothing, which will make the move of a set `noexcept` with every key
  again.
- The move constructor of a hash table copies its hash function, so that
  the table that it moves from keeps one that goes with its comparator.
  A pointer to a function, and a function object that fits in the buffer
  of a `std::function`, are copied without allocating; with one that
  does not fit, the move of a `LhashTable`, a `DynSetLhash`, a
  `DynSetHash`, a `DynMapHash` or an `ODhashTable` allocates once, in a
  constructor that is `noexcept`. Their move assignments copy nothing
  but a comparator that cannot be moved.
- MSVC moves the arguments of a constructor that a class inherits: with
  a comparator that can be copied and not moved, as one whose move
  constructor is deleted, it cannot build a `LhashTable`, a
  `LhashTableVtl`, a `DynLhashTable`, a `DynSetLhash` or a `DynMapHash`,
  however they are built, since they are built through the constructors
  that `LhashTable` and `LhashTableVtl` inherit. GCC, Clang and clang-cl
  build them.
- `Primes::next_prime()`, which the hash tables call with the count of
  buckets asked for, takes an `unsigned long`, of 32 bits on 64-bit
  Windows: a count of 2^32 buckets or more is cut there. And a count
  whose bytes fit in a `size_t` and that no machine has, as 2^59, still
  goes to it, which takes seconds, before `new[]` throws.
- The places where the result of a comparison is a condition inside a
  function that says `noexcept` are those that were found, and not a
  search of the library. The same holds for the tests of a range that a
  number that is not a number passes, as that of
  `Bloom_Filter::estimate()` was: other headers that take a probability
  or a density, as `random_graph.H`, `random_net.H` and
  `tpl_ca_stochastic_rules.H`, have them.
- `insertion_sort()` of an array loses an item when the comparison
  throws while it moves the items: the item that it took out is not put
  back.
- `Simplex::create_restriction()` loses the restriction that it
  allocated if its list has no memory for it.
- In `ArrayHeap`, with a comparison that may throw, a removed item that
  cannot be emptied without a throw stays in its entry, with what it
  holds, and so does the last item of the array with any comparison,
  until another item takes the entry or the array is destroyed.
- A move of the items of a `DynArrayHeap` that throws still leaves the
  heap out of order. When the key of `Fibonacci_Heap::decrease_key()`
  throws in its assignment, the node keeps what the assignment left, and
  may be out of order.
- In the interval tree, if the last assignment of a maximum throws, a
  move assignment that throws or the copy assignment of an endpoint that
  has no other, the maxima of some nodes may be left changed, though no
  node moves: the queries may then miss intervals, and the tree must be
  destroyed or built again.
- The iterators of the library keep their positions in a `long`, of 32
  bits on 64-bit Windows: on a container of 2^31 items or more, which
  `DynArray` admits, they would give a negative number of items. They
  will change together, in a later release.
- A splay tree builds a key by default in each search, insertion and
  removal, for the header node of its splay, inside `noexcept`: as
  `Splay_Tree` and `Splay_Tree_Rk` document, the default construction of
  the key must not throw.
