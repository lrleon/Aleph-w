# Aleph-w 7.2.2 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.2.2.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.2.2 is a patch release of the heaps that the graph algorithms
use, and of Kruskal's algorithm. Swapping two binary heaps, which moving a
`DynBinHeap` does, left each root pointing to the other heap. Moving an
`ArcHeap` read freed memory once the source was gone. Emptying an
`ArcHeap` or an `ArcFibonacciHeap` left the handles of its nodes, so
putting an arc to the same node again used a freed heap node. Emptying or
destroying a Fibonacci heap could overflow the stack. And Kruskal's
algorithm could not take a distance held in a variable, and a copy of it
used the distance of the original.

It is source compatible with 7.2.1, with a new requirement on the access
functor of the arc heaps ([Upgrade notes](#upgrade-notes)). As with every
release, recompile everything against the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.2.2.
2. `ArcHeap` and `ArcFibonacciHeap` call their access functor when they
   are emptied, by `empty()`, `clear()`, the destructors and the move
   assignments, to clear the handles of their nodes. The storage of the
   handles must then still be alive, and the functor must not throw,
   since the destructors and the move assignments do not throw. With the
   handles in a map, `operator[]` of `std::unordered_map`, for one,
   inserts the nodes that it does not find, and may throw
   `std::bad_alloc`. Prim, Dijkstra and A* meet both requirements.
3. `Kruskal_Min_Spanning_Tree` has a single constructor, which keeps
   copies of the distance and of the arc filter. No call could choose the
   one that took the distance by reference.

## Fixes

### Heaps

- **Swapping binary heaps.** `GenBinHeap::swap()` exchanged the roots of
  the two heaps but not the links from each root up to the header of its
  heap. `DynBinHeap` moves with `swap()`: a heap moved with more than one
  element failed an assertion when it extracted after its source was gone,
  and in a release build it went through the header of a dead object.
- **Moving an `ArcHeap`.** The move constructor kept the header and the
  root of the source, so the new heap read freed memory once the source
  was gone, and the move assignment did not compile. Both now swap the
  heaps.
- **Emptying the arc heaps.** `ArcHeap` and `ArcFibonacciHeap` deleted
  their nodes without clearing the handles that the access functor keeps
  for them, so putting an arc to the same node again used a freed heap
  node. They now clear the handles first, without comparing arcs. The
  move assignment of `ArcFibonacciHeap` also left the handles of the arcs
  of the target; it now empties the target first.
- **Emptying a Fibonacci heap.** `clear()`, the destructor and the move
  assignment of `Fibonacci_Heap`, and emptying an `ArcFibonacciHeap`,
  recursed once per level of the trees of the heap, and a tree can be as
  high as the heap is large: with six operations per node, the only tree
  of a heap becomes a chain, and a million nodes overflowed a stack of
  8 MiB. They now walk the trees without recursion and without allocating
  memory.

### Kruskal's algorithm

- **A distance in a variable.** With `dist` in a variable,
  `Kruskal_Min_Spanning_Tree<GT, Distance> kruskal(dist)` did not
  compile: its two constructors were equally good for it. Only a
  temporary worked.
- **Copies.** A copy of a Kruskal object used the distance of the
  original, through a reference, and read freed memory once the original
  was gone. Kruskal now keeps the distance by value, as Prim, Dijkstra,
  A*, Bellman-Ford and Johnson do, so a copy keeps its own, and the object
  can also be assigned.
