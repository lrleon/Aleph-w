# Aleph-w 7.3.5 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.3.5.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.3.5 is a patch release of the lists. `DynList` keeps the
number of its items, so that `size()` takes constant time and `split()`
walks only to the middle; `Dlink` splits a list without moving its
nodes, so that the merge sorts of `DynDlist` run 21 % to 28 % fewer
instructions; `DynList::Iterator::del()` keeps the item when its move
throws, where it lost it; and a delete through the iterators of
`DynListStack` and `DynListQueue` lowers their size, which it left one
more than their items.

It is source compatible with 7.3.4, but for the changes listed in the
[Upgrade notes](#upgrade-notes). As with every release, recompile
everything against the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.3.5.
2. A `DynList` keeps the number of its items, which its own operations
   update. Changing it through its base `HTList`, as with a call
   qualified with `HTList::` or through a reference to `HTList`, or
   through its nodes, as with `Slinknc::insert()` or `remove_next()`,
   does not update it, and leaves `size()` wrong.
3. `concat()`, `concat_list()`, `cut()`, `cut_list()`, `split()`,
   `split_list()` and `split_list_ne()` of `DynList` take a `DynList`,
   where they took an `HTList`; and `cut()` takes time linear in the
   items that it moves, which it counts, where it took constant time.
4. A `DynList` takes 24 bytes instead of 16, and a node of `List_SGraph`,
   which keeps its arcs in a `DynList`, 8 bytes more: 64 instead of 56
   with an `int`. The iterators keep their size, and so do `DynListStack`
   and `DynListQueue`.

## New

- `HTList::Iterator::get_list_ptr()`, protected, gives the list of the
  iterator to the iterators of the derived lists.

## Fixes

- `DynList::Iterator::del()` unlinked the node before it moved the item
  out, so that a move that threw lost the item and its node. The item
  moves out first, and the node leaves after it; if the move throws, the
  list keeps the item, and the iterator stays on it.
- A delete through the iterator of a `DynListStack` or a `DynListQueue`
  left its size one more than its items: they take their size from their
  list, which the delete lowers.

## Tests

- The tests check the count of a `DynList` after each operation that
  changes it, the halves of `split()` for 0 to 9 items, and 3000 random
  operations on three lists against a `std::vector` each and a recount
  of their nodes.
- A move that throws in `DynList::Iterator::del()`, on the first, a
  middle and the last item; the size of `DynListStack` and
  `DynListQueue` after a delete through their iterators; and the sizes
  of the rows of `in_place_transpose()`.
- The split of a list of `Dlink` for 0 to 9 nodes, linked both ways, and
  the sizes of the halves of `DynDlist`.

## Performance

Counted with cachegrind, with GCC 14 and Clang 22 at `-O2`, against
7.3.4:

- `DynList::size()` takes constant time; it walked the list.
- `DynList::split()`, with the join back, runs 79.2 % (GCC) and 88.6 %
  (Clang) fewer instructions on 100 000 items, and `mergeinsertsort()`
  of a `DynList<int>` 1.8 % and 1.4 % fewer.
- `DynDlist::split()` runs 75.7 % (GCC) and 79.5 % (Clang) fewer
  instructions on 100 000 items, and `mergesort()` and
  `mergeinsertsort()` of a `DynDlist<int>` 21 % to 28 % fewer, with 1000
  or 100 000 items. `sort_nodes()` and `sort_arcs()` of the graphs use
  these merge sorts.
- Where an item enters or leaves a `DynList`, the count costs up to
  0.8 %: an add or a subtract next to its `malloc()` or `free()`. The
  graph algorithms on `List_SGraph` and `List_Graph` run between 0.22 %
  fewer and 0.06 % more instructions, but the topological sort of
  `List_SGraph`, 0.29 % more with GCC; those that give the components as
  lists, 1.5 % to 2.5 % fewer.

## Documentation

- The documentation of `split_list()` of `Dlink` and `DynDlist` says
  that `l` takes the first n - n / 2 items; it said n / 2, and that
  `split_list_ne()`, which is `noexcept`, throws.
- Splitting a list of `Dlink` takes O(n), not O(1). The examples of
  `dlink.H` and `tpl_dnode.H` called `insert_next()` and `insert_prev()`,
  which `Dlink` does not have; and the comments of `insert_list()` and
  `append_list()` said the opposite of where they link the list.
- The comments of `dlink.H` that were in Spanish are in English.
- [`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md) lists 7.3.5.
