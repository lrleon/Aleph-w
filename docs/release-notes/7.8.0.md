# Aleph-w 7.8.0 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.8.0.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.8.0 is a minor release with a new header,
`Adaptive_Huffman.H`: adaptive Huffman coding of bytes in one pass,
with the updates of Faller, Gallager and Knuth (FGK). Its encoder and
decoder take their input in chunks of any size, read it once and send
no table of frequencies.

It also goes on with the work of 7.7.5 on what the containers do when
the code of the caller throws. `ArrayHeap`, `BinHeap`, `DynBinHeap` and
`Fibonacci_Heap`, and the arc heaps built on them, are now left as they
were when their comparison throws, where 7.7.5 left them out of order,
counted an item that they did not have or lost their minimum. So is
`DynIntervalTree`, which kept a freed node or left nodes hanging from
two parents. A Huffman encoder keeps its symbols when the generation of
its tree runs out of memory, and the move of a `BitArray` no longer
allocates.

It is source compatible with 7.7.5, but a node or an item whose
extraction or removal fails now stays in its heap (see upgrade note 3),
`update_key()` of `Fibonacci_Heap` keeps its node when the key
increases (see upgrade note 4), and the iterator of `DynIntervalTree`
loses a `prev()` that did not compile (see upgrade note 6). As with
every release, recompile everything against the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.8.0.
2. `Adaptive_Huffman.H` is installed with the other headers. It defines
   `Aleph::Adaptive_Huffman_Encoder` and
   `Aleph::Adaptive_Huffman_Decoder`. Its format is independent of that
   of `Huffman.H`.
3. When the comparison throws, a node or an item whose extraction or
   removal fails stays in its heap, and the heap is as it was. In 7.7.5
   it was out of the heap, and the heaps that own their nodes destroyed
   it, as their documentation said since 7.3. `DynBinHeap`, `ArcHeap`
   and `ArcFibonacciHeap` now free a node, or clear its handle, only
   once it is out. A node whose insertion in a `GenBinHeap`, the base of
   `BinHeap`, `BinHeapVtl` and `DynBinHeap`, fails is out of the heap,
   and can be inserted again.
4. `Fibonacci_Heap::update_key()` with a greater key keeps its node: it
   takes it out of its tree, puts it back as a root with the new key and
   returns the handle that it got. 7.7.5 deleted the node and inserted
   another one, whose handle it returned, so that a handle kept from
   before the call was left dangling. No node is allocated now.
5. `insert()`, `getMin_ne()` and `update()` of `GenBinHeap` are
   `noexcept` when neither the comparison nor the conversion of its
   result to `bool` in a condition can throw, where 7.7.5 looked at the
   call alone, and `ArrayHeap::remove()` asks the same before it takes
   its way with nothing to undo. `search()` of `DynIntervalTree` is
   `noexcept` only when its comparison and a copy of it do not throw,
   where it was `noexcept` with no condition: a comparison that threw
   there ended the program.
6. The iterator of `DynIntervalTree` loses `prev()`, which called a
   `prev()` that the iterator of the tree does not have: it compiled
   only where nothing called it.

## New

- **`Adaptive_Huffman_Encoder` and `Adaptive_Huffman_Decoder`.** Both
  start from a tree with the NYT leaf alone ("not yet transmitted") and
  update it after each byte. An unseen byte goes as the path of NYT and
  its eight bits, and a known byte as the path of its leaf.
- **Chunks.** `encode()` appends to a `BitArray`, one byte or a
  `std::string_view` at a time, and returns the number of bits that it
  appended. `decode()` reads a `BitArray` and appends each byte to a
  `std::string` when its last bit arrives, so that a chunk may end at
  any bit; `decode_bit()` takes one bit and allocates nothing.
  `finish()` checks that the message ended at the end of a codeword.
- **The model** has a fixed size, 513 nodes at most, and allocates
  nothing. An update costs O((h + 1) log 256), where h is the height of
  the tree. A copy owns its model.
- **Errors.** The decoder refuses a stream that is not well formed, an
  NYT for a byte already seen or with the whole alphabet seen, and asks
  for a `reset()` after it. So it does when `decode()` cannot grow its
  output: it has counted a byte that is not there, and going on would
  lose it unseen.
- **The stream** has no header, end marker or checksum: the caller
  keeps its exact number of bits, with `BitArray::save()` and `load()`
  for instance, or frames the transmission.
- **Two examples:** `adaptive_huffman_example`, three guided
  demonstrations in the terminal, and `adaptive_huffman_visual_example`,
  which writes an HTML gallery of the tree after each byte of a message.

## Fixes

- **heap** (#219): `ArrayHeap`, `BinHeap`, `DynBinHeap` and
  `Fibonacci_Heap` changed the heap, an entry of the array or a link of
  a node, before they finished comparing. With a comparison that throws
  at each of its calls in turn, `insert(1)` on an `ArrayHeap` that holds
  5 left the size at 2 and the top at 5, and `getMin()` on 1, 2, 3 and 4
  left the size at 3, lost the 1 and then gave 4, 2 and 3. On heaps of 8
  items, the 3 throws of an insertion in a `BinHeap` left the new item
  out of order, the 4 of an extraction lost the minimum, and the 2
  throws of an extraction of a `Fibonacci_Heap` lost its minimum. The
  sifts are those of 7.7.5, which exchange an item with its parent or a
  child as they compare. When the comparison throws, each exchange is
  undone by another one, which compares nothing, and so is what the
  operation did before the sift; `Fibonacci_Heap` puts its node back in
  the root list, with the minimum that it had, so that only its trees
  may be linked otherwise. A comparison that cannot throw leaves nothing
  to undo, and the compiler drops that code. The free functions
  `sift_up()`, `sift_down()` and `sift_down_up()` of `tpl_arrayHeap.H`
  do the same for a range. In `ArrayHeap` the guarantee holds if the
  exchange of two items does not throw; in the heaps of nodes it has no
  condition.
- **heap** (#219): `ArrayHeap::insert()` counted its item before it
  copied it, so that a copy that threw left one item more, and
  `getMin()` copied the minimum and then the last item over it, once it
  was counted out, so that a copy that threw lost the last item. They
  move the items now, and copy nothing.
- **heap** (#219): `verify_heap()` of `GenBinHeap` went down by the
  right child alone where a node had two. In heaps of 1 to 40 nodes in
  order, with one node made less than its parent, it said that 560 of
  the 780 were in order. It looks at every node now.
- **heap** (#219): `heapsort()` and `faster_heapsort()` moved their
  pointer one item back before they looked at the size. With nothing to
  sort the pointer may be null, and that offset is undefined. They
  return first now.
- **trees** (#217): `insert()`, `insert_dup()` and `remove()` of
  `DynIntervalTree` computed again the maximum endpoint of each subtree,
  with the comparison, after they had linked the nodes. A comparison
  that threw there left a freed node in the tree, in 7 of the 23 throws
  of an insertion on a tree of 6 intervals and in 12 of the 22 of an
  insertion with duplicates, or, in a removal, nodes hanging from two
  parents, which the destructor freed twice, or the node out of the tree
  and still counted. They now make every comparison, and every copy of
  an endpoint that may throw, before the tree changes, and leave the
  tree that the rotations left, node by node.
- **trees** (#217): `reset()` of the nodes of the interval tree, and
  their constructors from an rvalue, copy an endpoint and were
  `noexcept` with no condition: an insertion by move of
  `DynIntervalTree` with an endpoint whose copy throws ended the
  program. They are `noexcept` only when that copy cannot throw.
- **huffman** (#217): `generate_huffman_tree()` of a Huffman encoder
  took the two nodes of least frequency out of its heap before it made
  the node that joins them. When an allocation failed, the nodes that
  were out were lost, 1840 bytes in 37 allocations with three symbols
  and each of the seven allocations failing in turn, and the nodes of
  the symbols were freed while the map of the symbols still had them.
  The generation is now undone when an allocation fails: the encoder
  keeps its symbols and their frequencies, takes more, and generates the
  tree again. Without a failure the codes are those of 7.7.5. A single
  symbol gets its leaf in the tree of frequencies, where
  `get_freq_root()` threw.
- **bitarray** (#217): the move constructor of `BitArray` built an empty
  array first, which allocates the directory of its `DynArray`: one
  `malloc()` in a constructor that is `noexcept`, so that without memory
  the program ended. It now takes the bytes of its source by moving
  them, and allocates nothing.

## Performance

- **heap** (#219): cachegrind, at `-O2 -DNDEBUG`, counts the
  instructions per operation from 7.7.5 to 7.8.0, with 2 × 10^5 keys of
  `int` or 10^5 of `std::string`, whose comparisons cannot throw.
  `BinHeap` runs those of 7.7.5, exactly with Clang 18 and within 2 of
  them with GCC 14. `DynBinHeap`, and `Fibonacci_Heap` but in
  `update_key()`, run between 1.4 % fewer and 0.9 % more. `ArrayHeap`
  takes up to 2.1 % more, but in its insertions and removals of `int`
  with GCC 14: 38.49 → 42.05 (+9.2 %) and 65.43 → 68.92 (+5.3 %); the
  heapsorts, up to 1.4 % more. `update_key()` with a greater key saves
  the allocation of its node: 1719.36 → 1521.31 (−11.5 %) with GCC 14
  and 1709.54 → 1546.04
  (−9.6 %) with Clang 18 for `int`, and 3341.16 → 3177.61 (−4.9 %) and
  3282.78 → 3120.24 (−5.0 %) for `std::string`, whose copy of the key
  takes back half of that saving.
- **heap** (#219): with a comparison that the compiler cannot see, a
  function of another file, which may throw for all it knows, the
  undoing stays in the code. The largest costs are those of `ArrayHeap`
  with Clang 18: `remove()`, 107.02 → 133.12 (+24.4 %) for `int` and
  297.38 → 377.49 (+26.9 %) for `std::string`, `update()`, +11.8 % and
  +10.9 %, and `insert()` of `int`, +8.9 %. Elsewhere it is at most
  +8.0 %, in `DynBinHeap::remove()` of `int` with GCC 14.
- **trees** (#217): with 2 × 10^5 intervals of `int` endpoints, inserted
  at random and half of them removed, `insert()` goes 958.32 → 550.13
  instructions with GCC 14 and 978.02 → 746.76 with Clang 18, and
  `remove()` 846.79 → 463.43 and 994.79 → 606.17; with 10^5 of
  `std::string` endpoints, 7969.11 → 3721.22 and 9363.30 → 4153.92, and
  6964.56 → 2609.55 and 7989.55 → 2966.48. The operations no longer copy
  the comparator at each level, stop at the first maximum that does not
  change, and compare the two endpoints of an interval once.
- **bitarray** (#217): a move of a `BitArray` of 20 bits goes from
  647.21 to 39.01 instructions with GCC 14, and from 722.21 to 177.00
  with Clang 18. With it, the generation of the tree of a Huffman
  encoder of 256 symbols runs 2.4 % fewer instructions with GCC 14 and
  3.4 % fewer with Clang 18, with the same codes.

## Tests

- **heap** (#219): for each operation of the four heaps and each call of
  the comparison in turn, the heap after the throw is the heap of
  before, item by item, link by link in a `BinHeap` and node by node,
  with its minimum, in a `Fibonacci_Heap`. In 6000 operations of each,
  the operations of `ArrayHeap` and of `BinHeap` compare the same keys
  in the same order as those of 7.7.5 and leave each item at the same
  place, those of a same priority too. Other tests take a comparison
  that answers at random, a result whose conversions to `bool` differ,
  items with no default constructor and keys whose copy or assignment
  throws. 83 defects planted one at a
  time in the five headers of the heaps fail the tests, and the 59 test
  programs that include a heap pass under ASan and UBSan, 2950 tests.
- **trees** (#217):
  `IntervalTreeThrowingCompare.EveryFailedCallLeavesTheTreeAsItWas`
  makes each comparison throw in turn in 1119 insertions and removals,
  on trees of 0 to 12 intervals, and checks that every node is where it
  was, with its maximum and its priority.
  `IntervalTreeInsertionAndRemoval.SameTreesAsTheRotations` checks that
  the trees are those of the rotations of 7.7.5, and other tests take
  endpoints whose copies or moves throw. `template class
  Aleph::DynIntervalTree<int>;` does not compile with the `prev()` of
  7.7.5.
- **huffman** (#217): four tests of `huffman_allocation_test` make each
  allocation of the generation fail in turn. With the header of 7.7.5,
  three of them fail and the fourth ends in a heap-use-after-free.
- **bitarray** (#217): under ASan, the bytes that the program has
  allocated are the same before and after a move, which fails with the
  constructor of 7.7.5, and `BitArray` enters the typed suite of
  `moved_from_structures_test`.
- **huffman** (#213): the 19 tests of `adaptive_huffman_test` check the
  bits of known messages, round trips with every byte value and chunks
  cut at any bit, the invariants of the tree after each update of every
  sequence of up to 7 bytes of three values and of 6000 random bytes,
  codewords of 20 bits, and malformed and truncated streams. The 2 of
  `adaptive_huffman_allocation_test` make each growth of the output of
  `decode()` fail in turn. An independent review, with 76 mutations and
  an implementation of FGK of its own as an oracle, found no defect of
  the codec.

## Documentation

- **heap** (#219): the description of `tpl_binHeap.H` has a section on
  the representation of `GenBinHeap`, with a drawing: a complete binary
  tree of nodes and a circular list of the nodes that lack a child,
  which gives the parent of the next node to insert and the last node
  in O(1). A test checks each link of each node against it. The
  description no longer speaks of what the file does not have: a
  `BinHeapMax`, an example that did not compile, a `getMin()` of O(1)
  and a construction from n elements in O(n).
- **huffman** (#217): the documentation of the Huffman encoder says who
  frees its tree: `destroyRec(encoder.get_root())`, not the destructor.

## Known limits

- In `ArrayHeap`, with a comparison that cannot throw, a move
  assignment of an item that throws in `remove()` leaves the entry of
  the removed item as that assignment left it, where 7.7.5 lost two
  items. With a comparison that may throw, a removed item that cannot
  be emptied without a throw stays in its entry, with what it holds, and
  so does the last item of the array with any comparison, until another
  item takes the entry or the array is destroyed.
- A move of the items of a `DynArrayHeap` that throws still leaves the
  heap out of order, as in 7.7.5. When the key of
  `Fibonacci_Heap::decrease_key()` throws in its assignment, the node
  keeps what the assignment left, and may be out of order.
- In the interval tree, if the last assignment of a maximum throws, a
  move assignment that throws or the copy assignment of an endpoint
  that has no other, the maxima of some nodes may be left changed,
  though no node moves: the queries may then miss intervals, and the
  tree must be destroyed or built again.
- `BitArray::set_size()`, and `reserve()`, compute the bytes of one of
  the last seven sizes of `size_t` with a sum that wraps: the array
  frees its bytes and takes that size.
- `heapsort()` and `faster_heapsort()` still form a pointer to one item
  before the array when they sort two items or more.
- Some `noexcept` of the library, in the interval tree and in the graphs
  among others, look at the implicit conversion of the result of a
  comparison to `bool`, where the code makes the one of a condition: for
  a result whose two conversions differ, they may promise what the call
  does not keep.
- The iterators of the library keep their positions in a `long`, of 32
  bits on 64-bit Windows: on a container of 2^31 items or more, which
  `DynArray` admits, they would give a negative number of items. They
  will change together, in a later release.
