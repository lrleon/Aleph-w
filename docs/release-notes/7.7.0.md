# Aleph-w 7.7.0 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.7.0.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.7.0 is a minor release with a new header,
`Bounded_Cycle_Index.H`. `Bounded_Cycle_Index` enumerates once the
simple directed cycles of a digraph whose lengths are in a set, of 1 to
8 arcs, and keeps for each arc the cycles that go through it. When the
weight of an arc changes, it hands to a function the cycles of that arc
whose weight, the sum of those of its arcs, is below a bound. It is for
a graph whose shape stays while its weights change, as a graph of
markets, where a quote changes two arcs and only their cycles need to
be looked at again: a filter in `double` before an exact rule. On a
market graph of 19 nodes and 150 arcs, with its 61,646 cycles of 3 to 6
arcs, it filters the change of a market in 7.9 to 9.3 µs, where a
filter written by hand over contiguous arrays takes 20 to 22.

It is source compatible with 7.6.2. As with every release, recompile
everything against the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

   defines `Aleph::Bounded_Cycle_Index` and `Aleph::Arc_List_Order`.
   The rest of the library is that of 7.6.2: `min_cut()` of `tpl_net.H`
   loses the braces of a loop of one statement, and its code is the

## New

- **`Bounded_Cycle_Index<GT>`**, on a digraph of Aleph or on a
  `Const_Graph_View` of one. It uses no cookie, counter or bit of the
  graph, so that it works on a `const` graph. It keeps pointers to the
  arcs: the graph must outlive it, with the nodes and the arcs that it
  had when the index was built.
- **The catalog.** The arcs take the numbers 0, 1, ... in the order of a
  comparison of the caller; `Arc_List_Order`, the default, keeps that of
  the arc iterator. A cycle is the sequence of the numbers of its arcs
  from the least one: parallel arcs give different cycles, and a loop is
  a cycle of one arc. The cycles go by length and then
  lexicographically, so that those of at most `m` arcs are a prefix,
  with the positions that they have in the catalog of that smaller set,
  and a position identifies a cycle whatever the order in which the
  graph was built. `cycle_arcs()` gives the arcs of a cycle and
  `cycles_of()` the cycles of an arc, as `std::span`s.
- **The weights.** Each arc has a `double` that the index keeps apart
  from the graph: plus infinity for an arc that cannot be used, which is
  the weight of every arc until it is given one; NaN and minus infinity
  are rejected. The sum of a cycle is added from the weights of the
  moment, in the order of the cycle: it is the same number whichever arc
  changed, the one that `weight_sum()` gives, and no error is carried
  from one change to the next.
- **The filter.** `update(arc, w, bound, max_length, op)` writes a
  weight and calls `op(position, sum)` for each cycle of that arc, of at
  most `max_length` arcs, whose sum is below the bound. To change
  several arcs before any cycle is looked at, as the two arcs of a
  market, `set_weight()` writes each one and
  `for_each_cycle_below(arcs, bound, max_length, op)` hands once each
  cycle through any of them. `for_each_cycle_below(bound, max_length,
  op)` walks the whole catalog.
- **The margin.** The filter is exact for its weights. The description
  of the header says how to widen the bound so that no cycle that an
  exact rule accepts is lost, and why the bound must be rounded up;
  `sum_error_bound(k, max_abs_weight)` bounds the rounding of the sum of
  `k` weights.
- **A clock and a stamp by arc,** with `touch()` and `is_stale()`, for
  what the caller computes of a cycle and keeps.
- **The memory.** For each arc, the arcs of each of its cycles are
  contiguous, as numbers of 16 bits if the graph has at most 65,536 arcs
  and of 32 if not. The index is built in two passes, with a limit of
  cycles, 2^24 unless another is given: a graph with more cycles throws
  `std::length_error` before it takes their memory, and the peak of the
  build is what the index keeps.
- **Copies and moves.** A copy is another index of the same graph, with
  the weights, the stamps and the clock of the first. An assignment
  copies apart and then exchanges: if there is no memory for the copy,
  the index keeps what it had. A move takes an index in constant time
  and leaves its source without arcs and without cycles. `swap()`
  exchanges two indexes without failing: it is how to replace an index
  by another built apart, with another set of lengths.

## Known limits

- **Each cycle once costs more than it saves when few cycles are
  handed.** With the two arcs of each change of a market at once,
  `for_each_cycle_below()` looks at 45 % fewer cycles than two
  `update()`, but it reads a mark for each arc of each cycle, 4.0 ns a
  cycle against 1.6: on the recorded stream of the
  [Performance](#performance) it takes 35 to 39 % more. It pays where
  the function that receives the cycles is the expensive part.
- **The weights are a copy.** The index adds the weights that it keeps,
  which the caller writes with `set_weight()` or `update()`. A filter
  that walks the catalog of the index and reads each weight from its arc
  in the graph took five times more on that stream: each arc of a
  `List_Digraph` is an object apart.
- **The filter is in `double`.** It hands candidates, to be decided by
  an exact rule; the bound must be widened by a margin and rounded up.
  Where a sum is kept with more precision than a `double`, as with the
  x87 arithmetic of GCC 14 with `-mfpmath=387
  -fexcess-precision=fast`, the function can receive a cycle whose sum,
  as a `double`, equals the bound: one candidate more.
- **The sizes.** The lengths go from 1 to 8, and the nodes, the arcs and
  the arcs of all the cycles must be fewer than 2^32. The memory is
  O(E + C k^2) for `C` cycles of `k` arcs: 192 MB for the 948,614 cycles
  of 3 to 8 arcs of the market graph. The header needs a `size_t` of 64
  bits.
- **An index is not thread safe.** `for_each_cycle_below()` with arcs
  writes marks in it: queries from several threads need an index each,
  or a lock.

## Tests

- `Tests/bounded_cycle_index_test.cc`, 33 tests: the catalog against a
  search made apart, on 600 random multigraphs of each type of digraph
  (`List_Digraph`, `List_SDigraph`, `Array_Digraph`), with loops and
  parallel arcs, eight sets of lengths, four orders of the arcs, and
  through a `Const_Graph_View`; the catalog does not depend on the order
  of insertion; the counts of a complete digraph; the order and the
  prefixes; parallel arcs, loops and senses; the numbers of the arcs;
  `update()` against `weight_sum()`, with the same sum bit by bit
  whichever arc changed; each cycle once with several arcs, also with
  cycles of two arcs and with an arc given twice; the whole catalog; the
  empty ranges; what is not valid, and the limits; more arcs than fit in
  16 bits; a function that throws, also after an arc was looked at; the
  stamps; `swap()`, the copies and the moves, also a copy made inside a
  query and an index assigned to itself; only the loops, among many
  parallel arcs; and the bound of the error of a sum against exact
  rationals, with the bound rounded up.
- `Tests/bounded_cycle_index_allocation_test.cc`, 5 tests with a global
  `operator new` that fails on demand: the constructor throws
  `std::bad_alloc` whatever allocation fails, and frees what it took; an
  index built apart replaces another with `swap()` without allocating;
  an assignment that fails leaves the index as it was; and the queries
  and the moves allocate nothing.
- Of 71 mutants of the header, the tests catch 66; the five that survive
  change no result.
- They pass in the CI on Linux with GCC 11.4 and 13 and with Clang 14,
  with libc++, and 18, also with the sanitizers; on macOS; on Linux for
  ARM64; and on Windows with MSVC and clang-cl.
- On the recorded stream of the [Performance](#performance), the index
  decided as a filter over contiguous arrays in each of the 120 million
  cycles compared, with the fee of the markets and without fees. Without
  fees 667,105 were profitable by the exact rule, and none was lost.

## Performance

On a market graph of 19 nodes and 150 arcs, on a P core of a Core Ultra
7 265KF.

The build and the memory (Clang 18 / GCC 13 / GCC 14):

| Lengths | Cycles | Built in | Kept, which is also the peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 to 6 | 61,646 | 6.9 / 7.3 / 6.7 ms | 8 MB |
| 3 to 7 | 258,086 | 37 / 39 / 36 ms | 48 MB |
| 3 to 8 | 948,614 | 165 / 174 / 160 ms | 192 MB |

The filter ran as one more filter of a benchmark that replays a recorded
stream of quotes: 340 s, 42,109 changes of a market, each through 5,230
cycles on average. After each round of changes the benchmark evaluates
the exact rule, with rationals, and times eight filters in the order of
a row of a Williams square, so that each one runs once in each position
and once after each of the others. In µs per change of a market, with
the cycles of 3 to 6 arcs:

| Filter | GCC 14 | GCC 14, another run | Clang 18 |
|---|---|---|---|
| The exact rule | 5,380 | 5,331 | 5,368 |
| By cycle, over contiguous arrays, written by hand | 20.6 | 20.2 | 22.2 |
| **The index, an `update()` by arc** | **8.5** | **7.9** | **9.3** |
| — its percentile 99 | 13.7 | 11.8 | 14.0 |
| The index, `set_weight()` and `for_each_cycle_below()` | 11.5 | 10.9 | 12.9 |
| The catalog of the index, weights read from the graph | 44.2 | 42.7 | 48.9 |

On another stream, of 349 s, an `update()` by arc took 7.8 µs, and 11.1
at the percentile 99, with GCC 14.

With synthetic changes on the same graph, the two `update()` of the
change of a market take 3.9 to 4.2 µs with the memory warm, and 5.3 to
5.6 after walking 8 MB. Adding each sum in the order of the cycle, so
that it is the same whichever arc changed, costs 11 to 16 % with the
memory warm against adding the arc that changed first.
