# Aleph-w 7.8.1 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.8.1.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.8.1 is a patch release. It lifts the known limit of 7.8.0 on
`BitArray::set_size()`, whose bytes wrapped for the last seven sizes of
`size_t`, and fixes the same defect, a container that takes a size that
it cannot hold, in five more containers of arrays: `DynArray`,
`ArrayHeap`, `FixedQueue`, `FixedStack` and `MemArray`, and so in the
containers on them. Each one now refuses such a size with an exception
before it allocates, and is left as it was. The release also makes
`keys()` and `values()` of the maps, `ranks()` and `pair_ranks()` of the
lists and the copy of a `Random_Set` copy each item once.

It is source compatible with 7.8.0, but a size that a container cannot
hold now throws where 7.8.0 took it (see upgrade note 2), and a capacity
whose bytes do not fit in a `size_t` throws another exception (see
upgrade note 3). As with every release, recompile everything against
the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.8.1.
2. `DynArray` refuses an index past its limit, `Max_Dim_Allowed`, which
   is 2^38 entries, wherever it grows, as its constructor did:
   `touch()`, a write through `a[i]`, `reserve()` and `adjust()` throw
   `std::length_error`, and so do `append()` and `insert()` on an array
   that has `Max_Dim_Allowed` entries. 7.8.0 took the index, with a size
   of 2^38 + 1, or began to allocate every block up to it.
   `DynArrayHeap::reserve()`, on a `DynArray`, throws the same
   exception, and so do `BitArray::set_size()` and `reserve()` for more
   than 2^41 bits, whose bytes a `DynArray` cannot hold.
3. A capacity whose bytes do not fit in a `size_t` throws
   `std::overflow_error` before anything is allocated, in `MemArray`,
   and so in `Array`, `ArrayQueue`, `ArrayStack`, `FlatSet` and
   `FlatMap`, and in `ArrayHeap`, `FixedQueue` and `FixedStack`. 7.8.0
   left most of those capacities to `new[]`, which threw
   `std::bad_array_new_length` or `std::bad_alloc`, by the compiler and
   the items. Code that caught `std::bad_alloc` for them must catch
   `std::overflow_error` now. An `ArrayHeap` of `SIZE_MAX` entries and a
   `FixedQueue` of more than 2^63 throw it too, where 7.8.0 built them
   with a wrong capacity.
4. `FixedQueue(0)` has a capacity of 1. 7.8.0 took for it the logarithm
   of 0, whose conversion to a `size_t` is undefined.

## Fixes

- **bitarray** (#221): `BitArray::set_size()` computed its bytes as
  `(size + 7) / 8`, which wraps for the last seven sizes of `size_t`. An
  array that held `101` and got `set_size(SIZE_MAX)`, or
  `set_size(SIZE_MAX - 6)`, freed its bytes and took that size: then it
  read `000`. And `set_size(2^41 + 1)`, a byte more than a `DynArray`
  holds, began to allocate 256 GiB. `set_size()` now divides first, so
  that its bytes do not wrap, and a size whose bytes are more than
  `Max_Dim_Allowed` throws `std::length_error` before anything is
  allocated, in `set_size()` and in `reserve()`, with the array as it
  was (upgrade note 2). `operator|=` takes the bytes of the other array
  from `get_num_bytes()`, instead of the same sum, and
  `Adaptive_Huffman` no longer keeps its output seven bits below the
  maximum of `size_t`, which it did for that sum.
- **dynarray** (#221): the constructor of `DynArray` refused a
  dimension greater than `Max_Dim_Allowed`, but `touch(2^38)` and
  `a[2^38] = 7` returned, with a size of 2^38 + 1, and `reserve()` and
  `adjust()` to that size began to allocate every block up to it. With
  2^61, the directory asked for 32 TiB before it failed. The array now
  checks its limit where it grows its directory, the only way to an
  index that does not fit, before anything grows, and is left as it was
  (upgrade note 2).
- **heap** (#221): `ArrayHeap` allocates its capacity plus the entry 0,
  which a heap does not use, and `d + 1` wraps for `SIZE_MAX`: an
  `ArrayHeap<int>` built with `SIZE_MAX` allocated an array of no
  entries, and its first insertion wrote past it. `allocate_storage()`,
  which the constructor calls, now throws `std::overflow_error` before
  it allocates for a capacity whose array of `d + 1` entries does not
  fit in a `size_t`, with the heap as it was (upgrade note 3).
- **array** (#221): `FixedQueue` rounded its capacity up to a power of
  two with a shift by the logarithm of a `double`. The logarithm of 0 is
  `-inf`, which a `size_t` does not hold, and past 2^63 the shift is of
  64 bits or more: `FixedQueue<int>(2^63 + 1)` had a capacity of 1, and
  its second `put()` failed its assertion; without assertions, each item
  went to the same slot. `FixedQueue` now throws `std::overflow_error`
  for a capacity past 2^63, where no power of two fits, and takes its
  capacity from `std::bit_ceil()`, which gives 1 for 0 (upgrade notes 3
  and 4). The capacities of 1 to 2^63 are those of 7.8.0.
- **array** (#221): `MemArray`, `ArrayHeap`, `FixedQueue` and
  `FixedStack` left to `new[]` a count whose bytes do not fit in a
  `size_t`. GCC 14 throws `std::bad_array_new_length` then for items
  without a destructor, but for those with one, whose count `new[]`
  keeps before them, it asks the allocator for `SIZE_MAX` bytes, and
  Clang 18 does so for any item: `MemArray<std::string>::reserve(2^59)`
  threw `std::bad_alloc` after that request, and under AddressSanitizer
  the request ended the program. `Array` and `FlatSet` of
  `std::string`, on `MemArray`, did the same. They now throw
  `std::overflow_error` before `new[]` (upgrade note 3).

## Performance

- **maps** (#220): `keys()` and `values()` of `DynMapTree`,
  `DynMapHashTable` and `MapOpenHash`, and so of their aliases
  (`DynMapAvlTree`, `DynMapHash`, `MapODhash` and the others), took each
  pair by value: for each pair they copied its key and its value, and
  then the one that they return. They take each pair by reference now.
  cachegrind, at `-O3 -DNDEBUG`, counts per item, from 7.8.0 to 7.8.1,
  with 10^5 values of `std::string` of 30 characters, 573.50 → 370.50
  instructions with GCC 14 and 588.75 → 370.75 with Clang 18 for
  `keys()` of a `DynMapAvlTree<int, std::string>`, and 961.53 → 757.03
  and 969.78 → 771.78 for its `values()`. For a
  `DynMapHash<int, std::string>` they are 580.32 → 373.33 and
  590.48 → 375.48 for `keys()`, and 978.36 → 763.64 and 973.23 → 773.51
  for `values()`.
- **sort** (#220): `ranks()` and `pair_ranks()` of a list took each item
  by value before they appended it to their array, and `pair_ranks()`
  then copied it from that array to its pair. They take each item by
  reference now, and `pair_ranks()` moves the item of its own array to
  the pair. With the same strings in a `DynList`, `ranks()` runs
  2879.19 → 2680.88 instructions per item with GCC 14 and
  2771.01 → 2556.02 with Clang 18, and `pair_ranks()`, 3413.07 → 2782.41
  and 3293.06 → 2655.76.
- **random** (#220): the copy constructor and the assignment of a
  `Random_Set` took each item by value before they appended it. A copy
  of a set of those strings runs 204.00 instructions fewer per item with
  GCC 14, and 213.00 fewer with Clang 18.
- **array** (#221): the checks of the sizes are where the containers
  grow or allocate. cachegrind, at `-O2 -DNDEBUG`, counts
  118.14 → 122.95 instructions with GCC 14 and 106.70 → 110.70 with
  Clang 18 for a `BitArray::set_size()` up and down by one bit, and
  977.23 → 984.22 and 880.36 → 882.36 for a `FixedStack` of a capacity
  of 100 that the compiler does not know and its 100 `push()`. Such a
  `FixedQueue` and its 100 `put()` run 1260.22 → 1202.22 and
  1165.42 → 1110.36: `std::bit_ceil()` costs less than the logarithm.
  `BitArray::push()`, `touch()`, `append()` and `operator[]` of
  `DynArray`, `Array::append()` and a `FixedQueue` of a capacity that
  the compiler knows run the instructions of 7.8.0.

## Tests

- `Tests/size_limits_test.cc` is the test of the pattern. It gives
  `BitArray`, `DynArray`, `DynArrayHeap`, `ArrayHeap`, `MemArray`,
  `Array`, `ArrayQueue`, `ArrayStack`, `FixedQueue`, `FixedStack`,
  `FlatSet`, `FlatMap` and `SmallVector` the sizes that they cannot
  hold: those whose bytes do not fit in a `size_t`, near its maximum
  and from the least count that wraps, and, for the containers on a
  `DynArray`, those past its limit. Each one must refuse them before it
  allocates, with an exception that is not a failed allocation, and be
  as it was. The arrays, the queues and the stacks are given `int` and
  `std::string`. With any of the six headers of 7.8.0, a test fails.
- `Tests/bitarray.cc` and `Tests/dynarray.cc` have the cases of the two
  containers, with every function that takes the size or the index.
  They also check that the bits that come back after a `BitArray`
  shrinks to a multiple of 8 bits and grows again are 0.
  `Tests/fixedqueue.cc` checks the capacity of a `FixedQueue` for eight
  capacities from 0 to 1025, and the refusal past 2^63.
- `Tests/item_copies_test.cc` counts the copies of an item that counts
  them: one for each item of the new container, in `keys()` and
  `values()` of twelve maps (the seven tree maps, the four hash maps
  and `FlatMap`), in the ranks of a `DynList` and of a `DynDlist`, and
  in the copy and the assignment of a `Random_Set`. With each of the
  five headers of 7.8.0, the tests of its functions fail.

## Known limits

- `Static_Event_Table` and `Simplex` leave to `new[]` a count of the
  caller whose bytes may not fit in a `size_t`, as `MemArray` did, and
  the hash tables do the same with their buckets, after they round
  their length up to a prime.
- Some move constructors are `noexcept` but allocate, so that a move
  without memory ends the program instead of throwing `std::bad_alloc`:
  those of `DynSetTree` and `DynMapTree` on trees such as `Avl_Tree`,
  `Rb_Tree`, `Treap` and `Splay_Tree` when the key allocates as it is
  built by default, as `Array` does, and so that of
  `Gen_MultiPolynomial`; and, with any key, those of `DynSetHash`,
  `DynMapHash`, `MapODhash` and `MapOLhash`. That of `DynSetHash` and
  `DynMapHash` allocates a table of the capacity of the one that it
  takes.
- In `ArrayHeap`, with a comparison that cannot throw, a move
  assignment of an item that throws in `remove()` leaves the entry of
  the removed item as that assignment left it. With a comparison that
  may throw, a removed item that cannot be emptied without a throw
  stays in its entry, with what it holds, and so does the last item of
  the array with any comparison, until another item takes the entry or
  the array is destroyed.
- A move of the items of a `DynArrayHeap` that throws still leaves the
  heap out of order. When the key of `Fibonacci_Heap::decrease_key()`
  throws in its assignment, the node keeps what the assignment left,
  and may be out of order.
- In the interval tree, if the last assignment of a maximum throws, a
  move assignment that throws or the copy assignment of an endpoint
  that has no other, the maxima of some nodes may be left changed,
  though no node moves: the queries may then miss intervals, and the
  tree must be destroyed or built again.
- `heapsort()` and `faster_heapsort()` still form a pointer to one item
  before the array when they sort two items or more.
- Some `noexcept` of the library, in the interval tree and in the graphs
  among others, look at the implicit conversion of the result of a
  comparison to `bool`, where the code makes the one of a condition: for
  a result whose two conversions differ, they may promise what the call
  does not keep.
- The iterators of the library keep their positions in a `long`, of 32
  bits on 64-bit Windows: on a container of 2^31 items or more, which
  `DynArray` admits, they would give a negative number of items. They
  will change together, in a later release.
