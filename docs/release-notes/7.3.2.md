# Aleph-w 7.3.2 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.3.2.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.3.2 is a patch release of the graphs, the trees, the heaps and
the error macros. The depth-first searches of the graphs, the functions
of the trees of `tpl_tree_node.H` and the writers of `generate_tree.H` no
longer recurse, so that a graph with a path of 100 000 nodes, or a chain
of a million tree nodes, no longer overflows the call stack; and every
depth-first search is faster than in 7.3.1. It also fixes what the
reviews of 7.2.2 and 7.3.1 found, and what an audit of the graph layer
found: Kruskal's algorithm gave a wrong tree with `List_SGraph`, the
copies that several algorithms map to the graph did not map back to it,
`planarity_test()` left planar graphs as simple as a grid of 4 × 4 nodes
without an embedding, and a comparison that threw inside `DynBinHeap`
ended the program, among others.

It is source compatible with 7.3.1, but for the changes listed in the
[Upgrade notes](#upgrade-notes). As with every release, recompile
everything against the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.3.2.
2. The throwing macros of `ah-errors.H` (`ah_*_if` and `ah_*_unless`) no
   longer take a declaration as their condition, as in
   `ah_domain_error_if(auto p = find(x))`, and a message whose last `<<`
   gives `void`, which threw nothing, no longer compiles.
3. `List_SGraph::sort_arcs()` is gone: it could not sort the tree that
   keeps the arcs of the graph.
4. `remove_node()`, `Net_Graph::disconnect_arc()` and `clear_graph()` are
   no longer `noexcept`: the first two may throw `std::bad_alloc`, and
   `clear_graph()` what the removal of an arc throws.
5. `List_SGraph::insert_node(p)` and `Array_Graph::insert_node(p)` delete
   `p` if the set of nodes cannot take it, as `Net_Graph::insert_node(p)`
   did: a caller must not keep `p` in a `std::unique_ptr` through the
   call.
6. `Array_Graph::get_first_node()` and `get_first_arc()` throw
   `std::range_error` on an empty graph.
7. `Array_Graph::Node_Arc_Iterator` no longer derives from
   `Array_Iterator<void *>`; it has the same operations.
8. `Random_Graph::eulerian()` and `Random_Digraph::eulerian()` throw
   `std::domain_error` when the graph cannot be made Eulerian by adding
   arcs to it; they searched with no end, or aborted.
9. `insert()`, `update()`, `remove()` and `erase()` of `DynBinHeap` are
   `noexcept` only if the comparison cannot throw, so that what it throws
   reaches the caller; with `Aleph::less`, they still are.
10. After some algorithms, the cookies of the graph hold something else
    ([Mappings](#mappings)).

## New

- **`tpl_dfs_stack.H`**, with `Dfs_Stack`, the stack of the frames of a
  depth-first search that does not recurse, built in place, and
  `Dfs_Arc_Frame`, the frame of a search that keeps only the iterator
  over the arcs of a node. When a stack is destroyed, its thread keeps its
  room, up to 1 MiB, for the next one.
- **`Kept_Filter`**, in `tpl_graph.H`: a filter that an algorithm object
  keeps. One given as an lvalue is used where it is; one given as an
  rvalue is moved into the keeper, so that it lives as long as the
  object.
- **`AlephUncheckedIterator`**, in `ah-concepts.H`: an `AlephIterator`
  whose `get_curr_ne()` gives what `get_curr()` gives.

## Behavior changes

- The depth-first searches keep their frames in a stack of their own,
  and each thread keeps, after a search, a room of up to 1 MiB for the
  next one. They visit the nodes and arcs, and ask the arc filters, in
  the order of the recursion.
- Kruskal's algorithm no longer reorders the arcs of `g`, and with ties
  it gives the same tree on every run.
- The copy assignment of the graphs keeps the graph if it throws.
- Prim marks `Spanning_Tree` only the arcs of the tree.
- `compute_bipartite()` colors every component.
- Johnson gives `max()` for a node that the source does not reach, with
  integer distances, instead of 0.
- With parallel arcs, the flows of the arcs of a network may differ, but
  not their total. With floating flows, a flow that an augmentation
  leaves next to the capacity of its arc, or to zero, is set to it.
- `compute_blocks()` gives no empty graph at the end of its list, and a
  graph without cut nodes is one block. `Compute_Cut_Nodes::map_subgraph()`
  passes on what it throws; it emptied the copy and returned.
- `Find_Path_Depth_First` returns `false`, with an empty path, when there
  is no path, and `Path::remove_last_node()` removes the only node of a
  path without throwing.
- `Is_Graph_Acyclique` and `Has_Cycle` say that an empty graph is
  acyclic, and search with a filter other than `Dft_Show_Arc`;
  `is_graph_acyclique()` takes a loop of a graph of one node as a cycle;
  `Test_For_Path` and `is_graph_acyclique(g, start_node)` search in a
  graph with as many arcs as nodes.
- Twelve classes move into themselves a filter given as an rvalue
  ([Graph algorithms](#graph-algorithms)).
- `planarity_test()` gives an embedding for every planar graph, with
  `embedding_is_lr_linear`, and its rotations may differ from those of
  7.3.1.
- `graph_to_tree_node()` and `Graph_To_Tree_Node` no longer mark the arcs
  with `Convert_Tree`.
- `ArcHeap::get_distance()` and `ArcFibonacciHeap::get_distance()` give
  the distance of the comparison.
- `destroy_tree()` frees the nodes in another order, and leaves right the
  tree of the parent of the node that it destroys.
- `Tree_Node::traverse()` gives its operation a `Tree_Node *`.
- `are_tree_equal()` passes on a `std::length_error` that the comparison
  throws; it took it as a difference.
- `generate_tree()`, `generate_forest()` and `write_df_low_tree()` write
  trees of more than 1024 levels instead of throwing
  `std::overflow_error`.
- `mergesort()` keeps the order of equal items.

## Fixes

### Deep graphs and trees

- **The depth-first searches recursed** once per node of the path that
  they followed, so that a graph with a path of 100 000 nodes overflowed
  the call stack. They now keep their frames in a `Dfs_Stack`: the
  components of Tarjan and Kosaraju, the cut nodes and the bridges of
  `tpl_cut_nodes.H`, `Build_Subgraph`, `Find_Path_Depth_First`, the
  searches of `tpl_graph_utils.H`, `Topological_Sort`, the tests of
  cycles, acyclicity and paths, `Find_Depth_First_Spanning_Tree`,
  `graph_to_tree_node()`, `find_path_depth_first()`,
  `build_df_low_tree()`, the LR planarity test and the augmenting paths
  of `hopcroft_karp_matching()`.
- **The functions of `tpl_tree_node.H` recursed** as deep as the tree,
  and some of them along the siblings too: `traverse()` and the
  operations of `FunctionalMixin`, the traversals in preorder and in
  postorder, `clone_tree()`, `are_tree_equal()`, `compute_height()`,
  `deway_search()`, `search_deway()`, `forest_to_bin()` and
  `bin_to_forest()`. A chain of a million nodes, or a node with a million
  children for the last two, overflowed the stack. They no longer
  recurse, and they visit, compare and copy in the same order.
  `get_parent()` goes at once from a last child.
- **`destroy_tree()`** recursed as deep as the tree, and destroying a
  node with children while its parent lived corrupted the tree of the
  parent. The subtree now leaves the tree of the parent as if it had not
  been inserted, and its nodes are freed without recursion and without
  memory.
- **`generate_tree()`, `generate_forest()` and `write_df_low_tree()`**
  refused the trees of more than 1024 levels, and they and
  `generate_tree_graphviz()` wrote them by recursion. They all write
  trees of any depth without recursion.

### Graphs

- **Kruskal's algorithm** sorted the lists of arcs of `g`, and gave a
  wrong tree with `List_SGraph`, whose `sort_arcs()` could not sort the
  tree that keeps its arcs. It sorts a copy of the arcs, with a stable
  sort. Its tree version clears the cookies before it maps.
- **`Array_Graph::compress()`** and the shrinking of the arrays of arcs no
  longer call `realloc(p, 0)`, and they check what `realloc()` gives.
- **`List_SGraph::insert_arc()`** leaves the graph as it was if an
  insertion fails, and `insert_node()` counts the node once it is in.
  `remove_node()` collects the arcs before it disconnects them.
- **`Net_Graph`** takes `insert_arc(src, tgt)`, so that a network with an
  arithmetic `Arc_Type` can be copied; `insert_arc(src, tgt, cap, flow)`
  checks the flow before it inserts the arc; and `remove_arc()` and
  `disconnect_arc()` put the ends of the arc in the sets of sources and
  sinks before the arc goes.
- **The copy assignment of the graphs** copies and then swaps, so that
  without memory the graph keeps what it had, and it keeps its cookie.
- **The nodes and arcs:** the constructors that copy or move their info
  are `noexcept` only if that copy or move is; `get_control_bits()` gives
  the bits of the node; the `emplace_*()` functions forward their
  arguments; and the comparators of `sort_nodes()` and `sort_arcs()` do
  not bind temporaries.
- **`Digraph::swap()`** swaps only with a digraph.
- **`clear_graph()`, `remove_node()` and `Net_Graph::disconnect_arc()`**
  were `noexcept`, but may allocate: a network allocates when it removes
  an arc.

### Graph algorithms

- **`compute_blocks()`** read out of its array of blocks on a graph
  without cut nodes, and on the nodes of the components that
  `cut_nodes()` did not reach, and its list ended with an empty graph.
- **`Find_Path_Depth_First`** threw `std::underflow_error` when there was
  no path.
- **Twelve classes kept their filters by reference**, bound, when the
  filter was an rvalue, to a temporary that was gone when a named object
  ran: the default argument, for one. They keep them in a `Kept_Filter`:
  `Find_Path_Depth_First`, `Find_Path_Breadth_First`,
  `Directed_Find_Path`, `Is_Graph_Acyclique`, `Has_Cycle`,
  `Topological_Sort`, `Q_Topological_Sort`,
  `Test_Hamiltonian_Sufficiency`, `Test_Dirac_Condition`,
  `Test_Eulerian`, `Find_Eulerian_Path` and `Test_Single_Graph`.
  `Nodes_Index` and `Arcs_Index`, which call their filters only while
  they are built, no longer keep them.
- **Cycles and paths.** `Test_For_Path` said that there was a path in an
  undirected graph with as many arcs as nodes, and
  `is_graph_acyclique(g, start_node)` that the component of `start_node`
  had a cycle; such a graph need not be connected. The free functions
  `is_graph_acyclique()` said that a graph of one node was acyclic even
  if it had loops, and `Is_Graph_Acyclique` and `Has_Cycle` that an empty
  graph had a cycle and, with a filter that hides arcs, that a graph with
  as many arcs as nodes had one.
- **The embedding of `planarity_test()`** sorted the edges of each node by
  one key, which fails for planar graphs as simple as a grid of 4 × 4
  nodes, and then searched repairs and, if allowed, all the rotations:
  0.8 s to give up on that grid, 5 minutes on one of 40 × 40. It is now
  the embedding phase of the LR algorithm, as Brandes describes it, and
  every planar graph gets its embedding without repairs: the grid of
  40 × 40 takes 18 ms. The planarity answers do not change.
- **`graph_to_tree_node()` and `Graph_To_Tree_Node`** lost the tree built
  so far if the conversion threw, and a second conversion of the same
  graph stopped at the root. `graph_to_tree_node<GT, Key, Convert>()`
  compiles again.
- **Prim:** `prim(g, tree)` put a loop of the first node in the heap, and
  marked it `Spanning_Tree` in `g`.
- **Johnson** gave 0 for a node that the source does not reach, with
  integer distances, whose infinity is 0. It computes the distances with
  checked arithmetic and without building paths, and keeps the tree of
  the last source, so that the queries from it do not run Dijkstra again.
- **`compute_bipartite()`** colors every component, so that the matching
  works on graphs that are not connected.
- **The augmenting paths of the flows** take, with parallel arcs, the arc
  that they chose. With floating flows, rounding could leave a flow above
  the capacity of its arc, below zero, or next to zero, and then the
  checks of the network failed. Integer flows do not change.
- **Floyd-Warshall:** `Floyd_All_Shortest_Paths(g, {})` was ambiguous
  since 7.3.0; it takes again the constructor with the arc filter. The
  distance is no longer `mutable`: `get_min_path() const` measures the
  arcs with a copy of it when its call operator is not `const`, so that
  concurrent readers do not share its state.
- **The generators of `random_graph.H`** could not build one graph after
  another: a `Random_Digraph` over an undirected type of graph built
  undirected graphs from the second one on, the Eulerian graphs reached
  nodes of the former graphs, a Hamiltonian digraph built after an
  Eulerian one failed an assertion, and a graph that threw halfway stayed
  in the generator. And `eulerian()` searched with no end, or aborted in
  GSL, on graphs such as K4.

### Mappings

`map_nodes()` and `map_arcs()` compose with the mapping that a cookie
holds. These algorithms gave copies that did not map back to the graph,
because a cookie held something else or what a former run left:

- `Compute_Cut_Nodes`, whose cookies held the low values when it copied
  the blocks and the cut graph. After `cut_nodes()`, the cookies of the
  nodes are `nullptr`, and `map_subgraph()` and `compute_blocks()` can
  run again.
- `map_subgraph()` and `map_cut_graph()` of `tpl_graph_utils.H`.
- `Find_Breadth_First_Spanning_Tree` and
  `find_breadth_first_spanning_tree()`.
- `Tarjan_Connected_Components`, the arcs of the blocks.
- `kosaraju_connected_components()`, which left the nodes and arcs of `g`
  mapped to the transposed graph, which it destroys. With the list of
  blocks, the cookies of `g` are now `nullptr`; with the blocks, the
  nodes and the arcs inside a block map to those of the block, and the
  other arcs have `nullptr`.
- `Bellman_Ford::build_tree()`, whose cookies held the parents.
- `build_dominator_tree()`.

### Trees

- **`clone_tree()`, `forest_to_bin()` and `bin_to_forest()`** lost what
  they had made if a copy of a key threw, or memory ran out; they free
  it.
- **The operations of `FunctionalMixin` on `Tree_Node`**, such as
  `for_each()`, `all()`, `exists()`, `foldl()`, `maps()` and `filter()`,
  did not compile.

### Heaps

- **`DynBinHeap`** declared `noexcept` its `insert()`, `update()`,
  `remove()` and `erase()`, so that a comparison that threw there ended
  the program; and when the comparison threw, `getMin()`, `remove()` and
  the copy constructor lost memory. A node that a `GenBinHeap::remove()`
  that threw had taken out kept the control bits of its place, so that
  inserting it again failed an assertion.
- **`Fibonacci_Heap`:** `insert()` and `merge()` compare before they
  link, so that if the comparison throws the heap is as it was; if the
  comparison throws, `extract_min()` and `delete_node()` free the node
  that left the heap instead of losing it; and the consolidation
  allocates nothing, with its room reserved before the heap changes, so
  that a heap that was moved from fails cleanly without memory.
- **`ArcHeap` and `ArcFibonacciHeap`** keep a single distance, that of
  their comparison.

### Sorting

- **`mergesort()`**, of lists and of arrays, is documented as stable, but
  with equal keys its merges took first the item of the second half. The
  merge of arrays moves only the first half to its buffer, which needs
  half the room, and the merge sort of lists puts the items back in the
  list if the comparison throws.

### Error macros

- With a constant condition, Clang warned about `[[unlikely]]` on the
  `while` of the throwing macros (`-Wignored-attributes`, on by default).
  They are now a `for` that runs once.
- A message whose last `<<` gave `void` compiled and threw nothing, and
  with a constant condition the loop never ended. It no longer compiles,
  and one whose last `<<` gives a type convertible to `std::ostream &`
  compiles again.
- The conditions are converted with `static_cast<bool>`, so that the
  macros, and their `_constexpr` forms, take what `if` takes, but for a
  declaration.
- The macros can be arguments of other macros, such as `EXPECT_THROW`.

## Performance

Counted with cachegrind, with GCC 14 and Clang 22 at `-O2`, each
function built alone, against 7.3.1:

- Every depth-first search is faster, on a `List_Graph` and on an
  `Array_Graph`, with both compilers: from 0.06 % to 72.5 % fewer
  instructions. The iterator over the arcs of a node of an `Array_Graph`
  keeps four words instead of eight, and `Filter_Iterator::next_ne()`
  skips the checks that cannot fail.
- The functions of the trees: from 0.6 % (`generate_tree()`) to 95 %
  (`are_tree_equal()`) fewer; `destroy_tree()`, from 5.7 % to 8.8 %.
- `mergesort()` of arrays: from 26 % to 47 % fewer.
- Johnson, from 14 % to 99 % fewer; Ford-Fulkerson and Edmonds-Karp with
  integer flows, from 32 % to 43 %; `hopcroft_karp_matching()`, from
  7.5 % to 8.8 %; `planarity_test()` with the embedding, 29 % on small
  random graphs.
- Dijkstra, Prim, Kruskal, the breadth-first traversal and Bellman-Ford,
  which run over the iterators that changed, are faster too.
