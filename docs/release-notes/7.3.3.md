# Aleph-w 7.3.3 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.3.3.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.3.3 is a patch release of the graphs. It fixes what the review
of 7.3.2 found, and what two new kinds of tests found: exhaustive tests,
which check the graph algorithms against oracles on every small
multigraph of each kind of graph, with loops, parallel arcs, every order
of insertion and every set of hidden arcs; and a contract matrix, which
calls every template of the graph headers that takes an arc filter with
every kind of filter, also with filters that throw at each of their
calls. What a filter throws now reaches the caller of every graph
algorithm, where it ended the program; Dijkstra, A*, Bellman-Ford,
Johnson and the k shortest paths work near the limits of the type, as
the Upgrade notes say; the push-relabel flows give the maximum with
floating capacities; the arcs into a node of a digraph, which
`In_Iterator` did not give, have forms that take the graph, and an
index; and the Floyd-Warshall of `latex_floyd.H` takes the lightest of
the parallel arcs, among others.

It is source compatible with 7.3.2, but for the changes listed in the
[Upgrade notes](#upgrade-notes). As with every release, recompile
everything against the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.3.3.
2. The throwing macros of `ah-errors.H` and `parse_utils.H`, and the
   writers of `ah-errors.H` and `ah-trace.H`, take their condition as an
   `if` does: a scoped enumeration, as `ah_domain_error_if(e)` with an
   `enum class`, no longer compiles, which only 7.3.2 took; nor does
   `ah_bad_alloc_if(auto p = f())`.
3. `Aleph::less` and the other functors of `ahFunction.H` (`plus`,
   `minus`, `multiplies`, `divides`, `modulus`, `negate`, `equal_to`,
   `not_equal_to`, `greater`, `greater_equal`, `less_equal`,
   `logical_and`, `logical_or`, `logical_not`, and `unary_negate`) are
   `noexcept` only when the operator of their type is. The heaps, sorts
   and sets that compare with them pass on what that operator throws;
   their operations on types whose operators do not say `noexcept`, as
   `std::pair`, `std::tuple` or `std::vector` of libstdc++, are no longer
   `noexcept`.
4. From a node to itself, `Dijkstra_Min_Paths::find_min_path()`,
   `operator()` and `paint_partial_min_paths_tree()` give the path of no
   arc, of cost 0; they gave `max()` and no path.
5. `copy_painted_min_paths_tree()` of Dijkstra and A* copies only the
   nodes that the painting reaches, as `compute_min_paths_tree()` does.
6. With an integral distance, Dijkstra, A* and Bellman-Ford no longer throw
   `overflow_error` for a tentative sum beyond `max()`: only when the
   distance to a node that the start reaches does not fit. The methods of
   A* with a heuristic leave out an arc whose g + h does not fit: if the
   heuristic does not overestimate, no path through it fits, and with one
   that overestimates they can throw for an end whose distance fits.
   Yen throws only when the cost of one of the first k paths does not
   fit, and Eppstein when the cost of one of the first k walks, or the
   distance to the end of a node that reaches it, does not fit.
7. `Find_Eulerian_Path` says `Cycle`, with the empty walk, on a graph
   without arcs, as `Test_Eulerian` does; it said `None`.
8. `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph::non_feasible_nodes()` judges the flow of the
   auxiliary network, as `is_feasible()` does, and throws `domain_error`
   without it; both judged the fields `out_flow` and `in_flow`, which the
   network does not write.
9. `Lengauer_Tarjan_Dominators` keeps its computation in a cache, which
   its documentation now says: a change to the graph needs
   `invalidate()` (see New).
10. From a node to itself, `Find_Path_Breadth_First` and
    `Directed_Find_Path` give the path of no arc, and `Test_For_Path` says
    that there is a path, as `Find_Path_Depth_First`,
    `find_path_breadth_first()` and `test_for_path()` do; the first two
    found none, and `Test_For_Path` looked for a cycle. A start that meets
    the criterion is the path, as in `Find_Path_Depth_First`.
11. `Johnson` on a graph that is not directed takes each arc in both
    directions, so that an arc of negative weight is a negative cycle, and
    the constructor throws `domain_error`; its Bellman-Ford took each arc
    one way, and its Dijkstra both.
12. `Johnson` calls the filter and the distance on the arcs of the graph,
    once each, in its constructor, never on the copy that
    `get_reweighted_graph()` gives. There, an integer new weight beyond
    `max()` is `max()`, where the constructor threw. The queries throw
    `overflow_error` only for a pair whose distance is beyond `max()`.
13. `rank_graphviz()` gives the ranks of the arcs that its filter shows.
    `IO_Graph(g)` and `IO_Graph::set_arc_filter()` require a filter with a
    default constructor, and one that can be assigned: without them they
    fail at the call, not inside the header; one without them goes to the
    new constructor that takes the filter (see New).
14. The forms of the arcs into a node that take only the node,
    `In_Iterator(p)`, `in_degree(p)`, `in_nodes(p)`, `in_arcs(p)`,
    `in_pairs(p)`, `traverse_in_arcs()`, `for_each_in_arc()`,
    `all_in_arc()`, `exists_in_arc()`, `search_in_arc()`, `map_in_arcs()`,
    `foldl_in_arcs()` and `filter_in_arcs()`, no longer compile on a
    digraph type: the list of a node of a digraph holds only the arcs that
    leave it. The forms that take the graph, or `In_Arcs_Index`, give them.
15. On a digraph, `g.in_degree(p)`, `g.in_arcs(p)`, `g.in_nodes(p)` and the
    other `in_` members give the arcs into `p`; they gave only its loops.
    `GraphCommon::get_in_it(p)` is not `noexcept`; it and
    `In_Iterator(g, p)` throw `domain_error` on a graph of a type that is
    not directed and that says it is directed.
16. `prev()`, `prev_ne()` and `reset_last()`, and the `end()` of the graph
    iterators, do not exist on the iterators of `List_SGraph` and
    `List_SDigraph`, whose lists go one way.
17. `Path::insert(Node *)` and `Path::insert_directed(Node *)` throw
    `invalid_argument`, as their documentation said, and not
    `domain_error`.
18. With an integer cost type, `Hungarian_Assignment` throws
    `overflow_error` when the optimum does not fit in it, and, with
    `long long`, when the costs come near its limits; `int` keeps its
    potentials in `long long`, so that rows of costs from `max() / 2` on,
    which threw, give their optimum. `hungarian_max_assignment()` throws
    when the maximum does not fit.
19. What the filter of a `Filter_Iterator` throws goes out of `next()`,
    `next_ne()`, `prev()`, `prev_ne()`, the constructors, `reset_first()`
    and `reset_last()`, and so out of the graph iterators and of the
    algorithms that advance with them; `next_ne()` and `prev_ne()` are
    `noexcept` only when the filter is. `next_ne()` and `prev_ne()`
    swallowed every exception, and the others the `std::overflow_error` and
    `std::underflow_error` of the filter: the iterator stayed on the item
    on which the filter threw, and a loop took it as shown. As their
    filter, also `next_ne()` of `Out_Iterator` and `In_Iterator`, the moves
    of `In_Iterator(g, p, sa)`, `search_arc()`, `search_directed_arc()`,
    `search_spanning_tree_arc()`, `Bellman_Ford::clear()` and
    `check_painted_arcs()`, and the steps of the depth-first searches
    (Tarjan, the cut nodes, the components, Hopcroft-Karp, the topological
    sort, the spanning trees, `graph_to_tree_node()`, `Dfs_Arc_Frame` and
    those of `tpl_graph_utils.H`) are `noexcept`: they were always, and
    ended the program when the filter threw. Dijkstra and A* free their
    cookies without calling a filter that can throw, which they called
    again in a destructor: they mark with the bit `Min` the arcs that have
    one.

## New

- `In_Arcs_Index<GT, SA>` gives the arcs into each node of any graph: it is
  built once in O(V + E), and then gives the in-degree of a node in O(1)
  and an iterator on its arcs.
- The forms of the arcs into a node that take the graph, right on
  digraphs in O(E): `In_Iterator(g, p, sa)`, `in_degree(g, p, sa)`,
  `in_nodes()`, `in_arcs()`, `in_pairs()`, `traverse_in_arcs()`,
  `for_each_in_arc()`, `all_in_arc()`, `exists_in_arc()`,
  `search_in_arc()`, `map_in_arcs()`, `foldl_in_arcs()` and
  `filter_in_arcs()`; and the concept `DigraphType<GT>`.
- The filter object, where only its type could be given: the drawings of
  `generate_graph.H` and `generate_spanning_tree_picture.H`,
  `warshall_compute_transitive_clausure()`, `graph_to_tree_node()`, and
  the functions of `tpl_kgraph.H` and `Graph_Coloring.H` take it as their
  last parameter; `Ady_Mat`, `Bit_Mat_Graph`, `To_Graphviz`,
  `Generate_Graphviz`, `Compute_Min_Cut`,
  `Warshall_Compute_Transitive_Clausure`, `Stoer_Wagner_Min_Cut`,
  `IO_Graph` and the classes of the coloring take it in their
  constructors.
- `Lengauer_Tarjan_Dominators::invalidate()` drops the cache of the
  dominators.
- `Initialize_Dist`, `find_min_path()` and `Find_Min_Path` (`latex_floyd.H`
  and `mat_path.H`) take `Compare`, the comparison by which they choose
  the lightest of the parallel arcs.
- `nothrow_arc_filter_v<GT, SA>` says whether an arc filter is copied,
  moved and called without exceptions; then the searches that take it,
  as `search_arc()`, are `noexcept`.
- `Dfs_Stack::capacity()` and `release()`, and the helpers of the searches
  that keep their own stack (`Dfs_Here`, `dfs_here()`, `dfs_frame()`,
  `dfs_replace()`); `ALEPH_ANNOTATE_CONTAINERS` also poisons the free room
  of a `Dfs_Stack` under ASan.
- The exhaustive tests of the graph algorithms (`Tests/graph_exhaustive_*`)
  and the contract matrix of their filters (`Tests/graph_contract_*`),
  which calls again each filter whose call is not `noexcept`, throwing at
  each of its calls. `ALEPH_GRAPH_HARNESS` sets the size of their domains:
  `normal`; `light`, of one arc less, which a build with a sanitizer takes;
  and `heavy`, of one node and one arc more. Four jobs of the CI run them,
  and a weekly workflow their heavy mode.
- The CMake options `ALEPH_GRAPH_HARNESS_TESTS`, which builds those tests,
  and `ALEPH_COMPILE_CHECKS`, which runs the tests that compile code to
  check what the compiler says of it; they take minutes, and are off by
  default in a CI (the environment variable `CI`), on otherwise.

## What the notes of 7.3.2 said

- They said that `write_df_low_tree()` wrote trees of any depth without
  recursion: it still numbered the nodes by recursion (fixed below).
- They said that the sorts of lists kept their items when the comparison
  threw: not on every route, and `mergesort(DynList &)` lost items when
  the memory ran out (fixed below).
- They said that `insert()`, `update()`, `remove()` and `erase()` of
  `DynBinHeap` were `noexcept` only if the comparison could not throw,
  and that with `Aleph::less` they still were. `insert()`, `put()` and
  `append()` were never `noexcept` (they allocate), and, as `Aleph::less`
  was `noexcept` for every type, a `<` that threw still ended the
  program with the default comparison (Upgrade notes 3).
- They said that the macros take their condition as an `if` does: their
  `_if` forms took an `enum class` (fixed below).
- They said that rounding no longer broke the checks of the floating
  flows: `flow_value()` still compared their sums with `==` (fixed
  below).
- They said that the augmenting paths take the arc that they chose among
  parallel arcs: those built as a `Path<Net>` still took any of them
  (fixed below).
- They said that the copy assignment of the graphs keeps the cookie: that
  of `Net_Graph`, `Net_Cost_Graph` and `Net_Cap_Graph` lost it (fixed
  below).
- They said that a stack of a search keeps at most 1 MiB of room for its
  thread: `Tarjan_Connected_Components` kept the stack of its deepest
  search while it lived (fixed below).

## Fixes

### Error macros

- The `for` of one iteration of 7.3.2 hid that the statement throws: GCC
  and Clang warned of a missing return, a `[[noreturn]]` that returns, a
  division by zero or an index out of bounds that a constant condition
  guards, and `-Werror` failed. The throwing macros are a `while` again,
  with `[[unlikely]]` only for GCC, and the warnings are gone.
- The `_if` forms took an `enum class`, which `if` and the `_unless`
  forms reject.

### Heaps, sorting and lists

- `DynBinHeap` with `Aleph::less`: see Upgrade notes 3.
- `in_place_sort()` of lists was documented stable and was not: the
  insertion sort of its short lists put an item before the equal ones.
  Every sort of lists keeps the order of equal items.
- The template of templates of `mergesort()`, `mergeinsertsort()`,
  `in_place_sort()` and `sort()` of an rvalue list lost items if the
  comparison threw; `mergesort(DynList &)` allocated a node per item at
  each merge, and without memory lost items: it links the nodes again,
  allocates nothing, and the references to the items stay valid.
- The merge of arrays left moved items in its buffer if the comparison
  threw.
- `HTList::split_list()` gave one less than the number of items.
- `insertion_sort(DynDlist &)` did not compile.
- `DynDlist::remove_first_ne()` and `remove_last_ne()` copied the item in
  a `noexcept` function; they move it before its node leaves, once when
  the move can throw, so that if it throws the list keeps the item, and
  are `noexcept` as its move.
- `ArcFibonacciHeap::get_min_arc()` lost the handle of its node when
  `extract_min()` threw without memory; the constructors of
  `Fibonacci_Heap` reserved memory in `noexcept` functions; its
  constructors, moves and swap are `noexcept` as those of the comparison,
  and when the move of the comparison throws, the heap moved from keeps
  its elements.

### Trees

- `write_df_low_tree()` numbered the nodes with a recursion, one call per
  level, and kept the number of every node: 16 000 levels took 575 MB,
  and a deeper tree overflowed the stack. It numbers only the ends of the
  arcs that are not of the tree, in an iterative preorder; the output is
  the same.
- `Gen_Mo_On_Trees` on an empty graph did not reject a root of another
  graph.

### Maximum flows, costs and matching

- The push-relabel flows (FIFO, heap, random and HLPP) and `Min_Cut` gave
  less than the maximum when the source had an arc to the sink: the
  initial preflow made the sink active, and it gave its excess back.
- Dinic, the capacity scaling and HLPP returned the flow that they added
  to a previous flow, not the total, as their documentation says; a
  second run gave 0.
- With floating capacities, the push-relabel flows did not always end,
  left flows out of `[0, cap]`, aborted on their own checks, and lost a
  small flow beside a large one: with capacities far apart, as `1e15`
  next to `10`, they gave 0 or aborted. Each node judges its excess by
  its own flows, summed keeping what their rounding loses, and what the
  rounding leaves at the end goes back toward the source.
- `flow_value()` compared floating sums with `==`, so that it and
  `min_cut()` aborted on ordinary floating networks in a build with
  assertions; `check_network()` and they take the tolerance of
  `check_node()` of each node.
- `add_flow()` and `subtract_flow()` saturated or cleared arcs whose
  slack was exact: their tolerance was relative to the capacity, not to
  the error of the operation.
- The augmenting paths built as a `Path<Net>` took any of the parallel
  arcs, which could have no slack: with an antiparallel arc before, a
  path had slack 0, and a loop of the user did not end.
- `compute_maximum_cardinality_bipartite_matching()` built its network
  with `get_tgt_node()`: on a graph that is not directed, an arc inserted
  toward its node in `l` gave a loop, so that the matching was not
  maximum, or the flow threw `domain_error`.
- `make_super_source()` and `make_super_sink()` left nodes after a
  `bad_alloc`, and `unmake_super_source()` and `unmake_super_sink()`
  could remove a node of the user after a failure; the network keeps its
  super nodes, so that another call goes on.
- `noexcept` functions and destructors called removals that throw:
  `unmake_super_*()`, the guard of the super nodes and the destructor and
  the move of `Net_Cap_Graph`.
- `copy_graph()` and `inter_copy_graph()` of a network left nodes and
  arcs in the target if its cleanup failed too, and kept the auxiliary
  network of a `Net_Cap_Graph`, whose cookies pointed to nodes that were
  gone.
- The copy assignment of `Net_Graph`, `Net_Cost_Graph` and `Net_Cap_Graph`
  lost the cookie; that of `Net_Cap_Graph` did not give the strong
  guarantee.
- `Net_Cap_Graph`: Edmonds-Karp and then `update()` failed (SEGV), and
  `get_total_flow()` added the flow of every node.
- `Net_Sup_Dem_Graph` compared with capacities that nothing set,
  `is_feasible()` read flows that nobody wrote (Upgrade notes 8), its
  copies and its move lost the supplies, and `insert_node()` was ambiguous
  with Clang.
- `decompose_flow()` did not end with residues of rounding and lost a node
  of each cycle; it throws `domain_error` at a node that receives more
  than it sends.
- The successive shortest paths gave 0 on a second run, and did not give
  the strong guarantee that they documented; `solve_transshipment()`
  threw with another source or sink.
- The cycle canceling aborted, or did not end, with parallel arcs, by the
  witness of Bellman-Ford (below).
- The network simplex did not improve an initial flow, failed with a
  directed cycle, and aborted from a flow apart from the source.
- `solve_transportation()` compared floating totals with `!=`, so that
  `{0.1, 0.2}` to `{0.3}` was infeasible; `solve_circulation()` with
  demands or bounds, the baseball elimination with a team without margin
  and `design_survey()` with minimums gave wrong answers.
- `Hungarian_Assignment` read the column -1 when every cost of a row was
  `max() / 2` or more, and overflowed its potentials when the optimum
  did not fit (Upgrade notes 18); `hungarian_max_assignment<double>()`
  did not compile.
- `compute_maximum_cardinality_bipartite_matching()` wrote each node of
  the graph in its node of the network through the type of the nodes of
  the graph: on a `List_SGraph` it overwrote the link of the network
  node in the list of nodes, which the next node mended, so that a filter
  that threw in between left a network that crashed when destroyed; on a
  `List_Graph`, its counter.

### Shortest paths

- Bellman-Ford: `get_distance()` and `get_min_path()` added the weights
  from the end of the path, where a partial sum could overflow although
  the distance fits (undefined in `get_min_path()`); and `get_distance()`
  did not end after `build_tree()`, whose mapping it followed.
- The witness of a negative cycle is a cycle of the graph of the
  predecessors, given by the arcs that were relaxed: with parallel arcs
  it was a cycle of positive cost, with a parallel arc that the filter
  hides it took that arc, and with a filter that keeps only some arcs
  `has_negative_cycle(a)` said true with an empty witness, and the
  global search said false.
- The global operations of Bellman-Ford take every node as a source at
  distance 0, as a virtual source would: they no longer insert a node in
  the graph, which a reader at the same time saw; and an overflow of an
  integer distance threw from a `noexcept` function, which ended the
  program (SPFA lost memory then). A weight of `-inf` or `NaN` throws
  `domain_error`; `build_tree(tree, false)` without a painting followed
  stale cookies.
- `Dial_Min_Paths` sized its buckets by the weights of the hidden arcs
  too.
- The destructor of `Zero_One_BFS` read the graph: after the graph, a use
  after free, and before, it cleared the painting of another algorithm.
- Yen and Eppstein took a path whose cost is exactly `max()` for no path,
  and did not give it. Yen threw `overflow_error` when the search for a
  deviation from a node of a path reached the end only beyond `max()`,
  although the paths that were asked fit (Upgrade notes 6).
- Eppstein did not give the walks that go on from the target and come back
  to it, and in a digraph could take the heavier of two parallel arcs; it
  threw `overflow_error` on a parallel arc of weight near `max()`, and when
  the cost of a walk that was not asked, or that came after a cheaper one,
  did not fit (Upgrade notes 6).
- `Johnson`: the constructor threw `overflow_error` when a new weight or a
  sum on the way passed `max()`, although every distance fits; with
  `double` distances on `int` infos the new weights lost their fraction,
  and the distances were wrong; the filter was applied to the copy, which
  holds the new weights, and a distance that reads a map by arc threw
  `out_of_range`; on `List_SDigraph` a path could take a hidden parallel
  arc. Its documentation described a dummy node that it does not add.
- `latex_floyd.H` and `mat_path.H` took, of the parallel arcs between two
  nodes, the first one and not the lightest, as `Floyd_Warshall.H` did
  before 7.2.1: the distances and the paths could be longer than the
  shortest. They take the lightest by `Compare`; and the two headers can
  be included together.

### Searches, components and cuts

- A filter that cannot be assigned, as a lambda with captures or a
  filter with a reference or a `const` member, did not compile in the
  depth-first searches: `Find_Path_Depth_First`, `Depth_First_Traversal`
  and what uses it, the tests of cycles, paths and acyclicity,
  `Build_Subgraph`, `Compute_Cut_Nodes`,
  `Find_Depth_First_Spanning_Tree`, Tarjan (and so the negative cycles),
  Hopcroft-Karp and `graph_to_tree`. Nor did a filter whose move is not
  `noexcept`, as one with a copy written by hand or a `std::deque`.
- `inconnected_components()` and `build_subgraph()` stopped copying arcs
  when they met the last node of the graph: the last component lost
  arcs.
- `Tarjan_Connected_Components` kept the stack of its deepest search
  while it lived: after a deep `has_cycle()`, 95 MiB and a million copies
  of the filter.
- With `dlopen()` from a thread other than that of `main()`, that thread
  lost the room of its searches; and every unit that includes
  `tpl_graph.H` got a dynamic initializer.
- `Tarjan_Connected_Components::connected_components(g, blocks, arcs)`
  went over the blocks that the lists held before, and put in them arcs
  toward the blocks of g, or read a graph that had gone. The blocks and
  arcs of g go after them, which do not change.
- `Build_Subgraph` took the cookie of a node for its copy: after Tarjan,
  without a reset, it wrote arcs into the blocks of Tarjan or into freed
  memory. It does not read the cookies that the graph had, and each node
  and arc maps to its copy, not through them.
- `Graph_To_Tree_Node` judged the acyclicity without the filter, and
  rejected a tree that the filter gives; Hopcroft-Karp built the filter
  by default in each frame, so that the state of the filter of the user
  did not reach the search; `Tarjan_Connected_Components(SA)` was
  `noexcept` though it moved a filter whose move can throw.
- `Find_Path_Breadth_First` ignored the filter after the start, and
  `Directed_Find_Path::bfs()` failed an assertion when the start had a
  loop.

### Digraphs, iterators and filters

- On a digraph, `In_Iterator`, `in_degree()`, `in_nodes()`, `in_arcs()` and
  `in_pairs()` saw only the loops of a node: `Bidirectional_BFS` with
  `In_Iterator` did not find the paths of 4 nodes or more, and
  `MCF_Graph::demands_satisfied()` took the flow into a source for 0.
- `Filter_Iterator` kept the address of its container: a
  `Node_Arc_Iterator` built from a node pointer, or an iterator on a
  view, pointed to an object that had gone. It keeps a copy of a pointer
  and of the views that say so.
- `Out_Iterator::reset_first()` and `prev()` ignored the filter.
- `Filter_Iterator` took the item on which its filter threw as shown
  (Upgrade notes 19).
- `Copy_Graph` with a node filter aborted, or, without assertions,
  inserted an arc to a null node, as soon as an arc reached a node that
  the filter hides; it copies the arcs between the nodes that it shows.
- `Index_Graph::remove_node()` on a digraph left in its index the arcs
  that came into the node, which the graph freed: a later search read
  freed memory.
- `Bit_Mat_Graph::set_list_graph()` and the assignment of a graph kept the
  bits of the former graph, and the assignment of its own graph did
  nothing, so that it missed the arcs added since.
- `Ady_Mat::operate_all_arcs_list_graph(ptr)`, on a graph that is not
  directed, touched from the target of an arc its diagonal entry instead
  of that of the other end; the copy constructor of `Ady_Mat` threw
  `domain_error`, and its copies took none of the entries.
- `generate_net_spanning_tree()` did not compile.
- `Euclidian_Graph`: a copy lost the positions of the nodes; the copy of
  a `Euclidian_Digraph` freed each arc twice; its assignment did not
  compile; the assignments take a `const` graph.
- `quadnode.H` and `rbNode.H` defined `COLOR` differently, and
  `Dijkstra.H` took away the `PARENT` of `quadnode.H`.
- `Lengauer_Tarjan_Post_Dominators` said that it cached its computation;
  it computes it on every call.

### Euler, Hamilton and generators

- `Random_Graph::eulerian()` threw `domain_error` with graphs that can be
  made Eulerian, and on a digraph it was always wrong: it joins the odd
  nodes by a T-join of the complement of the graph, and on a digraph by
  paths of the complement.
- `Random_Graph` and `Random_Digraph` kept references to temporary
  initializers, as in `Random_Graph<G, Label> gen(42)`; a `const` one was
  modified through a `const_cast`, and a `static const` one failed
  (SEGV).
- With the same seed, `Random_Graph` and `Random_Digraph` gave another
  graph in another run on `List_SGraph`: `connect()` and `eulerian()`
  picked among the nodes in the order of their addresses. They follow the
  order in which they create the nodes.
- `Test_Eulerian` counted a loop once, and did not test the connectivity
  of a digraph; the conditions of Ore and Dirac counted the parallel
  arcs.

### Geometry

- `Rectangle::width()`, `height()`, `area()`, `perimeter()`, `center()`,
  `corners()` and `distance_squared_to()` allocate and were `noexcept`.

### Tests

- `Tests/random_graph.cc` is a GoogleTest program; it was a `main()` of
  asserts, which NDEBUG takes away.
- The tests include the headers through `-I`.

## Performance

Counted with cachegrind, with GCC 14 and Clang 22 at `-O2`, against
7.3.2, but for the times in seconds:

- With integral distances, Dijkstra and A* run from 0.6 % to 3.6 % fewer
  instructions, and Bellman-Ford from 14 % to 49 % fewer: the check of a
  sum is inlined and made once.
- Yen runs 10 % to 14 % fewer instructions (400 nodes, 2000 arcs,
  k = 8). Eppstein gives the walks that go on from the target, which
  costs it 17 % to 34 % more instructions when arcs leave the target,
  and 2 % to 4 % fewer when none does (400 nodes, 2000 arcs, k = 30).
- `Tarjan_Connected_Components::connected_components(g, blocks, arcs)`
  counted the blocks for each new one, and
  `Topological_Sort::perform<DynList>()` counted the list at each step:
  their time grew with the square of the blocks or of the nodes. 40 000
  nodes without a cycle took 6.2 s in the first, and take 21 ms; 20 000
  nodes took 516 ms in the second, and take 1.1 ms.
- On a digraph, `Bidirectional_BFS` with `In_Iterator` explores backward,
  as it did not: a search takes about 256 000 instructions instead of
  74 000 (300 nodes, 3000 arcs), of which 114 000 build the index of the
  arcs into each node. The `in_` members on a graph that is not directed
  take one or two instructions more per call.
- With integer costs, `Hungarian_Assignment` takes 1.3 % to 2.5 % more
  instructions; with `double`, the same or 0.5 % fewer.
- The flows with integer capacities take the same. With floating
  capacities, the preflows on a network of 300 nodes and 3000 arcs take
  3 % to 9 % fewer instructions, HLPP 11 % to 16 % more, and the preflows
  and HLPP on networks of 2 to 8 nodes 1000 to 3000 instructions more per
  call.
- `Johnson` builds with 41 % to 45 % fewer instructions, and answers its
  queries with about 85 % fewer: it reads the graph once, and runs its own
  Dijkstra on arrays, which allocates nothing. All the pairs take 73 % to
  75 % fewer.
- The filtered iterators move their base iterator without its checks once
  it has an item, in `next()` as in `next_ne()`. With Clang, a filtered
  loop takes 10 % to 66 % fewer instructions, and the depth-first searches
  up to 31 % fewer; with GCC, a loop with `next()` up to 81 % fewer, and
  the cut nodes on `Array_Graph` 17 % to 18 % fewer. With GCC, the
  topological sort takes 2.7 % to 2.9 % more, the searches of Tarjan up
  to 1.3 % more, and a breadth-first search on `List_Graph` 1.0 % more;
  with Clang, a filtered `Array` with `next_ne()` 5.6 % more.
- `floyd_all_shortest_paths()` runs 0.3 % to 3.9 % fewer instructions;
  `find_min_path()`, which looks at every arc of a node to take the
  lightest, 3.7 % to 15.6 % fewer on sparse graphs and 7.9 % to 59.3 %
  more on dense ones.
