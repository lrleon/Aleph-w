# Aleph-w 7.7.5 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.7.5.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.7.5 is a patch release. It lifts the known limit of 7.7.4 on
`DynArrayHeap`: when its comparison throws in an insertion or an
extraction, or an insertion runs out of memory, the heap is now left as
it was, where 7.7.4 left it out of order, lost its minimum or counted an
item that it did not have. It also bounds the iterator of the heap by
its items, where it went through the entries that `reserve()` made, and
fixes `DynArray`, which lost a new block, or kept it half filled, when a
copy of its default value threw.

It is source compatible with 7.7.4, but the iterator of `DynArrayHeap`
is now a class of its own, which no longer derives from that of
`DynArray` (see upgrade note 2), and the insertions of a copy need the
copy constructor of the items (see upgrade note 3). As with every
release, recompile everything against the new headers
([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.7.5.
2. The iterator of `DynArrayHeap`, which its `Const_Iterator`,
   `get_it()` and the range `for` use, goes through the items of the
   heap, the entries 1 to `size()` of its array, as `traverse()` does,
   and its positions are theirs, from 0. It is now a class of its own,
   with the members that it had, and no longer derives from
   `DynArray<T>::Iterator`: it does not convert to it, and its
   `Set_Type` is the heap, where it was the array.
3. `insert()` and `insert_direct()` of a copy build a copy of the key,
   with the copy constructor of the items, and compare it before they
   move it into the array, where 7.7.4 assigned the key to an entry of
   the array. The items must thus be copy constructible to be inserted
   by copy.

## Fixes

- **heap** (#215): `sift_up()` and `sift_down()` exchanged the item
  with its parent or its child at each level, so that a comparison that
  threw on the way left the item halfway. `getMin()` had already taken
  the minimum out, which was lost, and `insert()` counted the item
  before it made its place and copied it, so that without memory for
  either, the heap counted an item that it did not have. With a
  comparison that throws once, `insert(1)` on a heap that holds 5 left
  the size at 2 and `top()` at 5, and `getMin()` on 1, 2, 3 and 4 left
  the size at 3, lost the 1, and the next extractions gave 4, 2 and 3.
  The sifts now make every comparison first, keeping the places on the
  way, and then move each item of the way to the place of the one
  before it, so that if a comparison throws, nothing has moved. The
  insertions of a copy compare the copy before they place it, and count
  it last. An insertion by move makes the place of the key and moves
  the key, as 7.7.4 did, and then compares with what it moved: if a
  comparison throws, the key takes it back by a move assignment.
  `getMin()` places the last item from the root before it moves
  anything. The same holds for `put()` and `append()`, which are
  `insert()`, and for `get()` and `getMax()`, which are `getMin()`. The
  comparisons and the final places are those of the exchanges, also for
  a key that is an item of the heap itself.
- **heap** (#215): the iterator of `DynArrayHeap` took the size of its
  array as its end, not the number of items. `reserve()` makes that size
  the reserved one, and `insert_direct()` does not change it: after
  `reserve(10)` and three `insert_direct()`, the iterator visited 9
  entries, and an insertion by move whose key threw when it was moved
  left one entry more to visit. `reset_first()` left the iterator
  without a current item, `reset_last()`, `end()` and `is_last()` went
  to the end of the array, and `set_pos(get_pos())` moved it one item
  back (upgrade note 2).
- **array** (#215): `DynArray` gave a new block to the array before it
  filled it with the default value. When a copy of that value threw, in
  `touch()`, in `reserve()` or in a write through `a[i]`, the block of a
  new segment was lost, with its items alive, and that of a segment that
  existed stayed in the array half filled. The block is now filled
  first, and released if a copy throws.

## Performance

- **heap** (#215): cachegrind, at `-O2 -DNDEBUG`, counts per operation,
  from 7.7.4 to 7.7.5, 188.53 → 163.85 instructions with GCC 14 and
  205.83 → 194.82 with Clang 18 for `insert()` of a copy of 5 × 10^5
  random `int`, and 1475.44 → 1354.31 and 1362.74 → 1309.45 for their
  `getMin()`: an item moves once per level, where an exchange moved it
  three times. An insertion by move of 2 × 10^5 random `std::string`
  runs 428.54 → 429.44 and 490.70 → 492.77.
- **array** (#215): touching 5 × 10^6 `int` entries of a `DynArray`, one
  after another, runs 5.6 % fewer instructions with GCC 14 and 10.7 %
  fewer with Clang 18, and touching 10^6 `std::string` entries, 7.3 %
  and 4.6 % fewer: the block is filled through a local pointer.

## Tests

- `DynArrayHeapThrowingCompare.EveryFailedCallLeavesTheHeapAsItWas`
  makes the comparison throw at each call, in turn, of `insert()` and
  `insert_direct()`, of a copy and of a move, and of `getMin()`, on
  heaps of 0 to 40 items whose identity a move takes away. Every item
  stays in its place, the size is the same, and the key of a move has
  its item. With the header of 7.7.4 it fails.
- `SameComparisonsAndPlacesAsTheOldSifts` checks, in 5000 random
  insertions and extractions with many equal priorities, that the two
  items of each comparison and the item at every place are those of the
  exchanges of 7.7.4. Three more tests check a key that is an item of
  the heap, copied or moved, also with a comparison that throws.
- `dynarrayheap_allocation_test` makes each allocation of `insert()`
  fail in turn, on heaps of 0 to 70 `std::string` and on one whose next
  item needs a second block of the array. With the header of 7.7.4, its
  first case ends the program in an assertion of `DynArray::access()`.
- The three tests of `DynArrayHeapIterator` go through every member of
  the iterator on heaps never used, reserved, filled and emptied, and
  on an iterator built without a heap.
- `DynArrayBlocks.ABlockThatCannotBeFilledLeavesTheArrayAsItWas` makes
  each copy of the filling of a block throw in turn, for `touch()`,
  `reserve()` and the three ways of writing through `a[i]`. With the
  header of 7.7.4 it fails for the five, and LeakSanitizer reports the
  lost blocks.

## Known limits

- `DynIntervalTree::insert()` and `insert_dup()` still free a node that
  they left linked when the comparison throws after the link, in the
  update of the maximum or in a rotation, so that the tree keeps a freed
  node, as in 7.7.4.
- A move of the items of a `DynArrayHeap` that throws still leaves the
  heap out of order, in its insertions and its extractions, and ends the
  program in `sift_up()` and `sift_down()` when the comparison does not
  throw. When the comparison throws in an insertion by move, the key
  takes its item back by a move assignment; if that assignment throws
  too, the item is lost.
- The iterators of the library keep their positions in a `long`, of 32
  bits on 64-bit Windows: on a container of 2^31 items or more, which
  `DynArray` admits, they would give a negative number of items. They
  will change together, in a later release.
