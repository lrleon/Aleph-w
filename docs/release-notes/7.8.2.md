# Aleph-w 7.8.2 release notes

Language: English | [Español](7.8.2.es.md)

Aleph-w 7.8.2 is a patch release. It fixes a pattern that the Coverity
Scan of 7.8.0 reported in the move of a `DynSetTree`: a function that is
`noexcept` and allocates, or builds an item of the user, ends the
program when there is no memory, or when that item throws, where it
should let the exception through. The pattern was in the moves of the
sets and the maps on trees and on hash tables, of `FixedQueue` and of
`DynDlist`, and in the constructors of the binary heaps and of
`DynMatrix`. The moves that built something for the container that they
moved from take what that one had now, and allocate nothing; and those
that must build an item are `noexcept` only when that construction is.

It is source compatible with 7.8.1, but a set or a map on a tree is not
nothrow move constructible any more with a key or a value whose default
construction may throw (see upgrade note 2), and a moved-from
`FixedQueue` or hash table of chained lists has no capacity (see upgrade
notes 3 and 4). As with every release, recompile everything against the
new headers ([`VERSIONING.md`](../../VERSIONING.md#abi-compatibility)).

## Upgrade notes

1. `find_package(Aleph 7 ...)` accepts 7.8.2.
2. A `DynSetTree`, a `DynMapTree` or an `AHMapping` is nothrow move
   constructible only when the default construction of its key, and of
   the value of a map, is `noexcept`. Their move builds an empty tree
   for the set that it moves from, whose header nodes hold a key built
   by default, and it was `noexcept` whatever that construction threw.
   It is still `noexcept` with `int`, `std::string`, `std::vector`,
   `std::map`, a pair of them, a class whose default constructor is the
   implicit one, and the containers of Aleph that allocate nothing when
   they are built: `DynList`, `DynDlist`, and a set or a map on a tree
   that is not randomized. It is not with a class whose default
   constructor is written by hand without `noexcept`, nor with a
   container that allocates when it is built: `Array`, `DynArray`,
   `BitArray`, the hash tables, and a set on `Treap`, `Treap_Rk` or
   `Rand_Tree`. So a `Gen_MultiPolynomial`, whose keys are arrays, is
   not either. A `std::vector` copies such sets and maps when it grows,
   where it moved them (see [Performance](#performance)), and the
   functions of Aleph that look at the move of an item, as
   `DynList::remove_ne()`, take their path for a move that may throw. To
   keep the move, declare the default constructor `noexcept` when it
   cannot throw. The move assignment and `swap()` build nothing and stay
   `noexcept`, and `Splay_Tree_Rk`, which has no header node, keeps its
   `noexcept` move with every key.
3. A moved-from `FixedQueue` has a capacity of 0, as a moved-from
   `FixedStack`: it must be assigned another queue before it takes an
   item. 7.8.1 gave it a queue of 1024 items.
4. A moved-from `DynSetLhash`, `DynSetHash` or `DynMapHash` has a
   capacity of 0 until a key enters it, and then the default one. 7.8.1
   gave it the capacity of the table that was moved from it. A
   moved-from `LhashTable` or `DynLhashTable`, which could not be used,
   takes keys again: with its own hash function after a move
   construction, and with the one that the other table had after a move
   assignment.
5. The constructors of `BinHeap`, `BinHeapVtl` and `DynBinHeap`, the
   default constructor, the moves and `swap()` of `DynMatrix`, and the
   move of a `DynDlist` are `noexcept` only when the default
   construction, or the move, of their item is. They were `noexcept`
   without a condition.

## Fixes

- **tree** (#223): the move constructor of a `DynSetTree`, `noexcept`,
  builds an empty tree for the set that it moves from, so that it stays
  usable, and the header nodes of that tree hold a key built by default.
  With a key whose default construction throws, as one that allocates
  does without memory, the program ended; so it did in the move of a
  `DynMapTree`, of an `AHMapping` and of a `Gen_MultiPolynomial`. The
  `noexcept` of the move now follows the construction of that tree: the
  default construction of the key and of the comparison, and the copy of
  the comparison. What they throw reaches the caller, with the set as it
  was (upgrade note 2). So that the sets and the maps whose keys and
  values cannot throw keep their `noexcept` move, the node of a tree
  looks into a `std::pair`, the key of the nodes of a map, whose default
  constructor libstdc++ does not declare `noexcept`; and `Dft_Pair_Cmp`,
  the default constructors of `DynList`, `DynSetTree` and `DynMapTree`
  and the constructor of `Splay_Tree_Rk` are `noexcept` when what they
  build cannot throw. `Dft_Pair_Cmp`, `DynSetTree` and `Splay_Tree_Rk`,
  which took their comparison as a default argument of their
  constructor, have a default constructor of their own for it: GCC and
  Clang do not count a default argument in the `noexcept` of the
  implicit default constructor of a class that inherits such a
  constructor.
- **hash** (#223): the move of a `DynSetLhash`, a `DynSetHash` or a
  `DynMapHash` built, for the table that it moved from, a table of its
  capacity, in a constructor that is `noexcept`: the move of a
  `DynSetHash<int>` of 10^6 keys allocated 18.5 MB. It now takes the
  lists and allocates nothing. The table that it moves from is left
  without lists, with capacity zero, and keeps its hash function, its
  comparison, its load thresholds and its resizing policy; the first key
  that enters it gives it the lists of the default capacity (upgrade
  note 4). `GenLhashTable` is valid without lists: its searches find
  nothing, its insertions and `resize()` make the lists,
  `current_alpha()` is 0, and `stats()` gives an average and a variance
  of 0. Its move constructor copies the hash function and the
  comparison, as that of `ODhashTable` does, instead of moving them, and
  its move assignment frees what the table holds and swaps the two
  tables, so that it copies nothing. With a comparator that can be
  copied and not moved, as one whose move constructor is deleted, which
  `std::swap()` does not take, the move constructor and `swap()` of
  `LhashTable` and `DynLhashTable`, and the moves and `swap()` of
  `DynSetLhash`, which did not compile in 7.8.1, compile now: `swap()`
  swaps such comparators through their copies.
- **hash** (#223): an insertion in a `DynSetLinHash` or a
  `DynMapLinHash` did not free the bucket of the key when there was no
  memory for its list. `DynHashTable` and `DynLhashTable` free it now,
  in a linear table and in one of chained lists whose lists are made by
  that insertion. And `swap()` of a linear table, `noexcept`, exchanged
  the arrays of the two tables through a third `DynArray`, which keeps a
  default value of its own, a list header with a key built by default;
  so did its move assignment, which swaps. It calls the `swap()` of
  `DynArray`, which builds nothing.
- **array** (#223): the move of a `FixedQueue` built a queue of the
  default capacity, 1024 items, for the queue that it moved from,
  whatever the capacity of this one: 4096 bytes for `int`, and 1024
  items built by default for any other type. It now takes the array, and
  leaves the other queue empty and without array (upgrade note 3). The
  iterator of a queue took the index of the youngest item modulo the
  capacity, a division by zero for such a queue; it takes it with the
  mask.
- **list** (#223): the move of a `DynDlist` built the header node of the
  new list, which holds an item built by default. For an item whose
  default construction may throw, the header node now takes the place
  of that of the other list, and its item by a move: no item is built,
  and the move is `noexcept` when that of the item is. If that move
  throws, the other list keeps its items. For the other items the move
  is that of 7.8.1, and so it is for an item that cannot be moved, as
  one whose move constructor is deleted.
- **heap** (#223): the constructors of `BinHeap`, `BinHeapVtl` and
  `DynBinHeap`, `noexcept`, build a header node whose key is built by
  default. Their `noexcept` now follows the default construction of the
  node and the copy of the comparison, as that of the trees does
  (upgrade note 5). That copy is the one that the constructor makes,
  from a comparison that is not const, and so it is in `Dft_Pair_Cmp`
  and in `Splay_Tree_Rk`.
- **matrix** (#223): the default constructor, the move constructor and
  the move assignment of `DynMatrix`, `noexcept`, build a default value
  by default. Their `noexcept` and that of `swap()` now follow the item,
  and the moves build the default value before they change anything, so
  that if its construction throws, the matrices are as they were
  (upgrade note 5). `swap()` calls the swap that its `noexcept`
  evaluates: that of the item found by its arguments, and `std::swap()`
  when it has none.

## Performance

- **hash** (#223): the hash function of the pairs of `MapODhash`,
  `MapOLhash`, `HashMap`, `DynMapHash` and `DynMapLinHash` was a
  `std::bind` of `map_hash_fct()`, which does not fit in the buffer of
  the `std::function` of the table: each copy of it allocated, and a
  move copies the functions. The maps now give their table a function
  object that holds only the pointer to the hash function of the keys.
  A `std::function` keeps it in its buffer, so that its copies do not
  allocate, and each hash calls the pointer, where it built a
  `std::function` first.

The searches and the insertions of the tables of chained lists ask
whether the table has lists: up to 9 instructions more. The maps on
hash tables save those of the `std::function` that each hash built. The
moves of the sets and the maps on trees cost what they did. The last row
is the price of upgrade note 2: the `std::vector` copies each map, with
its 16 nodes, where it moved it.

## Tests

- `Tests/noexcept_allocation_test.cc` is the test of the pattern. It
  builds, moves, move assigns and swaps each container, with `int` and
  with an item whose default construction allocates, and counts the
  allocations of `operator new` of what is `noexcept` among them: there
  must be none. And the container that was moved from must take items
  again, unless it is one of a fixed capacity. It goes through the
  lists, the arrays, the stacks, the queues, the heaps, the sets and the
  maps on the fourteen trees and on the hash tables, the mapping, the
  polynomial of several variables and the graphs.
- `Tests/noexcept_construction_test.cc`, which checks on every tree that
  the `noexcept` of what builds a type of the user follows that type,
  goes from 67 tests to 176. It takes `Splay_Tree_Rk`, and the sets and
  the maps on each of the fourteen trees: they are built and moved
  without throwing with types that cannot throw; their move is not
  `noexcept` with a key, a value or a comparison whose default
  construction may throw, nor with a comparison whose copy may; what
  they throw reaches the caller, with the set or the map as it was; and
  so does what the default construction of the comparison throws when a
  class that inherits the constructors of a set is built by default. It
  also checks the nodes of a pair, `Dft_Pair_Cmp`, the maps of
  containers and `AHMapping`.
- The tests of each container have its cases: the move that builds no
  item, the one that throws and the one of an item that cannot be moved
  in `Tests/dyndlist.cc`; the queue that a move leaves without capacity
  in `Tests/fixedqueue.cc`; the moves of a matrix whose default value
  cannot be built, and the swap of an item that has one of its own, in
  `Tests/dynmat_test.cc`; the `noexcept` of the heaps in
  `Tests/binheap.cc`; the tables that were moved from, which take keys
  again with their hash function and their load thresholds, in
  `Tests/dynsethash.cc` and `Tests/hash-it.cc`, which also swaps and
  moves them with a comparator that can be copied and not moved, and
  their statistics in `Tests/hash_chain_stats_test.cc`; the insertions
  without memory in `Tests/hash_allocation_failure_test.cc`; and the
  hash of the pairs in `Tests/dynmapohash_test.cc`.
- With any of the 18 headers whose code changes taken back to 7.8.1, a
  test fails; and each of 85 mutations of the fixes, one at a time,
  makes a test fail or not compile.
- Every test program of `Tests/` runs under ASan and UBSan with GCC 14:
  467 programs, with 16 754 tests in the 464 of GoogleTest. The 24
  programs of the containers that change run with the UBSan of Clang 18.
  A sample of 34 programs compiles with GCC 11 and with Clang 14 and
  libc++, the compilers of ubuntu-22.04 in the CI, and adds no kind of
  warning to those of 7.8.1 with GCC 14 and Clang 18. The traits that
  the tests expect hold with GCC 11, 13 and 14 and with Clang 14 and 18,
  with libstdc++ and with libc++.
- The platform workflow passes: on Windows with MSVC, which runs 13 485
  tests, and with clang-cl, and on macOS and on ARM64 Linux.

## Known limits

- On `Treap`, `Treap_Rk` and `Rand_Tree`, the tree that the move of a
  `DynSetTree` builds for the set that it moves from makes its generator
  of random numbers, a block of `malloc()` of 2528 bytes, with any key.
  The `noexcept` of the move does not count it: without memory for it,
  the program ends. And the move of a `DynSetLinHash` or a
  `DynMapLinHash` builds a linear table, whose `DynArray` makes its
  directory with `malloc()`, and a default value that holds a key built
  by default. Both are left for 7.9.0, with a move of a tree that builds
  nothing, which will make the move of a set `noexcept` with every key
  again.
- The move constructor of a hash table copies its hash function, so
  that the table that it moves from keeps one that goes with its
  comparator. A pointer to a function, and a function object that fits
  in the buffer of a `std::function`, are copied without allocating;
  with one that does not fit, the move of a `LhashTable`, a
  `DynSetLhash`, a `DynSetHash`, a `DynMapHash` or an `ODhashTable`
  allocates once, in a constructor that is `noexcept`. Their move
  assignments copy nothing but a comparator that cannot be moved.
- With a comparator that can be copied and not moved, as one whose move
  constructor is deleted, the move assignment and `swap()` of
  `ODhashTable` and `OLhashTable`, `swap()` of `LinearHashTable`, and
  the moves and `swap()` of `DynSetLinHash` do not compile, as in 7.8.1:
  they swap the comparator with `std::swap()`. And MSVC, which moves the
  arguments of a constructor that a class inherits, cannot build with
  such a comparator a `LhashTable`, a `LhashTableVtl`, a
  `DynLhashTable`, a `DynSetLhash` or a `DynMapHash`, however they are
  built: they are built through the constructors that `LhashTable` and
  `LhashTableVtl` inherit. GCC, Clang and clang-cl build them.
- The copy of a `LinearHashTable` itself, which the compiler generates,
  shares the buckets of the other table: destroying the copy is a
  use after free. `DynSetLinHash` and `DynMapLinHash`, whose copies
  insert the keys in a table of their own, are not affected.
- A tree that inherits its constructors and takes its comparison as a
  default argument, built by default with a comparison whose default
  construction throws (`Avl_Tree<int, Compare> tree;`), ends the
  program: GCC and Clang take its implicit default constructor as
  `noexcept`. The sets, the maps and their moves do not go through that
  constructor.
- `Static_Event_Table` and `Simplex` leave to `new[]` a count of the
  caller whose bytes may not fit in a `size_t`, as `MemArray` did, and
  the hash tables do the same with their buckets, after they round
  their length up to a prime.
- In `ArrayHeap`, with a comparison that cannot throw, a move
  assignment of an item that throws in `remove()` leaves the entry of
  the removed item as that assignment left it. With a comparison that
  may throw, a removed item that cannot be emptied without a throw
  stays in its entry, with what it holds, and so does the last item of
  the array with any comparison, until another item takes the entry or
  the array is destroyed.
- A move of the items of a `DynArrayHeap` that throws still leaves the
  heap out of order. When the key of `Fibonacci_Heap::decrease_key()`
  throws in its assignment, the node keeps what the assignment left,
  and may be out of order.
- In the interval tree, if the last assignment of a maximum throws, a
  move assignment that throws or the copy assignment of an endpoint
  that has no other, the maxima of some nodes may be left changed,
  though no node moves: the queries may then miss intervals, and the
  tree must be destroyed or built again.
- `heapsort()` and `faster_heapsort()` still form a pointer to one item
  before the array when they sort two items or more.
- Some `noexcept` of the library, in the interval tree and in the graphs
  among others, look at the implicit conversion of the result of a
  comparison to `bool`, where the code makes the one of a condition: for
  a result whose two conversions differ, they may promise what the call
  does not keep.
- The iterators of the library keep their positions in a `long`, of 32
  bits on 64-bit Windows: on a container of 2^31 items or more, which
  `DynArray` admits, they would give a negative number of items. They
  will change together, in a later release.
